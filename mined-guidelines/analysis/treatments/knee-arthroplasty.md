# Knee arthroplasty: four payers compared

Documents: Florida Blue 02-20000-60 (rev. 05/15/26, 8 pp), Kaiser NCP 114 (eff. 2/4/2026, 6 pp), BCBS Rhode Island Total Joint Arthroplasty (eff. 03/01/2025, 9 pp, hip and knee), Independent Health M20210611026 (eff. 9/1/2021, 9 pp). Profiles are in `sourcing/analysis/profiles/`.

## What it is

Knee arthroplasty replaces the diseased surfaces of the knee with metal and polyethylene parts. It comes as total (TKA, CPT 27447), unicompartmental or partial (UKA, 27446), and revision of an earlier replacement (27486, 27487). Payers treat it as elective and gate it on a common core. The core is (1) a diagnosis, usually osteoarthritis, but also avascular necrosis, tumor, fracture, rheumatoid arthritis, or a failed prior procedure; (2) imaging proof of advanced disease (Kellgren-Lawrence grade, "bone on bone", Outerbridge IV, or listed findings); (3) pain or functional loss that limits daily activity; (4) a documented trial of non-surgical care, usually 3 months; and (5) an absence of contraindications such as infection. Some payers add patient-optimization gates (BMI, HbA1c, tobacco). Florida Blue adds none of the optimization gates.

## Agreement and disagreement

Where they align:
- All four require imaging evidence of advanced disease and a trial of non-surgical care, and all four exclude active infection.
- Avascular necrosis, tumor or malignancy, fracture, and failed prior procedures are alternative routes in three or four of them.
- 3 months is the common conservative-care benchmark (Florida Blue, Kaiser, Independent Health). BCBSRI states it as over 12 weeks of PT and activity modification.
- Kellgren-Lawrence appears in Florida Blue (UKA only), Kaiser, and Independent Health.

Where they differ:
- Optimization gates (BMI, HbA1c, tobacco) are hard cutoffs at BCBSRI (BMI under 40, A1c under 8.1, 4 weeks off tobacco). They are soft or conditional at Kaiser (A1c 7.5, BMI 40 with review) and Independent Health (documentation of effort or counseling). They are absent at Florida Blue.
- Imaging bar: Kaiser and Independent Health require severe disease (K-L 3/4 or IV, Outerbridge IV). BCBSRI accepts bone-on-bone or 2 of 5 findings. Florida Blue accepts any single listed finding for TKA.
- Conservative therapy: Florida Blue needs only one modality of eight. Kaiser needs a medication trial plus 3 PT sessions. BCBSRI needs all three of NSAID/PT/activity modification, in the last year. Independent Health needs two or more strategies with dated documentation.
- Revision criteria exist at Florida Blue, BCBSRI and Independent Health but not Kaiser.
- Unique to one payer: BCBSRI age 18 or older and a lab-score infection pathway; Kaiser caregiver/home and non-ambulatory exclusions, PT 3 sessions in 12 months, and medical director review at BMI over 40; Independent Health 1/4-mile walking distance, 6-month visit recency with attestation, dialysis/transplant exclusion, and a no-deadline rule; Florida Blue UKA goniometer arc and deformity limits (shared in part with Independent Health).

Florida Blue stricter than the others:
- Only on UKA conservative care: Florida Blue requires all five elements (activity modification, drugs, supervised PT, assistive device, injections), which is more than any other payer's UKA or TKA bar.
- Florida Blue is stricter than the others on nothing else in TKA. For UKA it adds geometric limits (arc over 90 degrees, no ACL instability, deformity caps) that Independent Health also has and Kaiser and BCBSRI lack.

Florida Blue looser than the others:
- No BMI, HbA1c, tobacco, or age gate (BCBSRI has all four; Kaiser has BMI and A1c; Independent Health has soft versions).
- TKA conservative care is any one of eight modalities, with no PT requirement (Kaiser requires PT sessions; BCBSRI requires PT or home exercise over 12 weeks).
- TKA imaging accepts any one finding, no K-L grade (Kaiser and Independent Health require severe grade).
- No stated symptom severity, walking distance, or visit-recency rule.
- Exclusions list is short (4 items), versus Kaiser's social and rehabilitation exclusions and Independent Health's long medical-risk list.

## Structure

- Layout: Florida Blue is a prose Description (a long UpToDate quote) followed by a Position Statement of nested bullets with footnotes. Kaiser is a short policy with a line-of-business table, numbered criteria, grading tables, and an Exclusions section. BCBSRI is a lettered pathway outline covering hip and knee, with item counts such as "(a,b,c,d,e,f,g,h)". Independent Health is a checkbox-headed corporate form with an ALL-of-procedure structure, documentation block, and NOT COVERED lists.
- Logic shape: Florida Blue is a flat OR-then-AND list. Kaiser is ALL of 6 with k-of-n leaves, plus an ALSO pathway. BCBSRI is 5 pathways, each ALL, up to 5 levels deep. Independent Health is a top-level ALL with a choice of procedure and an OR of three documentation branches.
- Where criteria live: Florida Blue puts contraindications in a footnote. Kaiser uses a separate Exclusions section and a separate grading table. BCBSRI embeds exclusions as positive requirements in every pathway. Independent Health puts NOT COVERED lists after the criteria.
- Delegation: Florida Blue to LCD L33618 (Medicare), FEP and SAO guidelines. Kaiser to LCD L36575 (note the reference list cites L36577 and L36007) and a level-of-care policy. BCBSRI to Medicare Advantage policies; InterQual is cited only as a reference. Independent Health to an LCD/LCA and to self-funded plan documents.
- Codes: Florida Blue lists 5 CPT codes, Kaiser 12, BCBSRI 3 knee plus 5 hip. Independent Health lists none. No ICD-10 anywhere. No document maps codes to individual criteria.

## Problems for an automated checker

- Florida Blue TKA grouping is ambiguous: "Failure of a previous osteotomy, OR ... Avascular necrosis of the knee, OR Proximal tibia fracture, OR Advanced joint disease ... AND Pain or functional disability ... AND Failure of at least 3 months of conservative ... AND There are no contraindications*". A parser must choose whether the AND bullets apply to the six diagnoses or only to the imaging bullet. The lead-in "one or more" suggests the former for the OR diagnoses, but the layout is flat.
- Florida Blue UKA nests a contraindication list inside conservative therapy ("None of the following contraindications ... are present" as a sixth bullet).
- Florida Blue Description holds clinical statements ("under the age of 50", "weighing less than 82 kg") that are not criteria but will be picked up by keyword extraction.
- Kaiser: "BMI < 40: if BMI is > 40" leaves BMI exactly 40 uncovered, and "relative contraindication" conflicts with a hard threshold. "If diabetic the HbA1c < 7.5 ... if A1C >7.5" leaves 7.5 uncovered; "active medical management pre and post operatively" cannot be shown before surgery; "heart disease" is undefined.
- Kaiser PT: "at least 3 Physical Therapy sessions in the last 12 months ... OR documentation that Physical Therapy is not clinically appropriate" requires a chart to show absence of a clinical judgment.
- BCBSRI: the conservative-therapy block follows "AND must meet ALL the following" under option 2 ("no bone-on-bone contact, but has at least 2 of the following 5 findings"), so it is unclear whether bone-on-bone patients skip it. "intra-articular corticosteroid injection > 1 injection" means at least 2.
- BCBSRI: "Tobacco use: 4 weeks off of tobacco use prior to scheduled surgery" is a future fact on a pre-op chart. The infection pathway is a run-on sentence ("... or without prosthetic joint infection by positive synovial fluid culture or tissue culture and ALL of the following (a, b, c, d) AND") with mis-lettered items. "Prior authorization is recommended" conflicts with the policy statement.
- Independent Health: "BMI is less than 40 and/or they have no medical comorbidities" makes the leaf nonsensical for a strict tree. The A1c line "(> 8.5%)" sets when optimization must be documented, not a cutoff. "when indicated" and "reasonable" are unmeasurable. The effective date (9/1/2021) conflicts with references accessed August 2026.
- Shared vague terms: "failure" of conservative care, "reasonable activity restrictions", "as appropriate", "severe", "significantly increase the risk of morbidity". A chart cannot show "failure" without a stated outcome measure.
- Criteria a chart cannot show: tobacco abstinence before a date not yet reached, "active medical management ... post operatively", "surgeon has reviewed the increased risks" (Independent Health), "Lack of caregiver or unsuitable home situation" (Kaiser).

## Test cases

1. 58-year-old, K-L grade 2 osteoarthritis, only one knee finding (osteophytes), 4 months of NSAIDs, BMI 33, no PT, no tobacco, A1c normal. Florida Blue: approves (one finding, one modality of the eight, no contraindications, bullet "Advanced joint disease demonstrated by ... periarticular osteophytes"). Kaiser: denies (K-L 2 is below Grade 3; no 3 PT sessions). BCBSRI: denies (needs bone-on-bone or 2 of 5 findings, and PT/activity modification over 12 weeks).
2. 62-year-old, K-L 4 bone-on-bone, 6 months of PT and NSAIDs, BMI 44, smoker, A1c 9.0 (diabetic). Florida Blue: approves (no BMI, tobacco or A1c criteria). BCBSRI: denies (BMI < 40, 4 weeks tobacco-free, HgbA1C < 8.1 are required in every pathway). Kaiser: not an automatic approval (BMI over 40 needs optimization documentation and medical director review; A1c over 7.5 needs active management). Independent Health: BMI over 40 requires 6 months of non-surgical care, which is met, so conditional approval with documented optimization effort.
3. 70-year-old with severe unicompartmental medial arthritis (K-L 4), ACL intact, arc 100 degrees, 3 months of medications and injections only, no assistive device, no supervised PT. Independent Health: approves partial replacement if documentation meets the 2-or-more strategies rule (medication and injections are two). Florida Blue: denies UKA because it requires ALL of activity modification, drugs, supervised PT, assistive device and injections. Kaiser: denies (fewer than 3 PT sessions, unless PT is documented as not clinically appropriate).
