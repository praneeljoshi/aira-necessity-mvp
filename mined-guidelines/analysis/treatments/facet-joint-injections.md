|---|---|---|---|

| **Carelon** | Carelon Clinical Appropriateness Guidelines, Musculoskeletal: Interventional Pain Management (facet section pp.13-18 of 34); this copy is a redline posted by Louisiana Medicaid | Effective 04/14/2024 (old 04/09/2023 also printed) | 34 |
| **Evolent** | Evolent 2026 Interventional Pain Management Guidelines; facet content is CG 1753 (injections and blocks) and CG 1754 (denervation), PDF pp.31-44 (CG 1753) and 45-56 (CG 1754) of 102 | Effective July 1, 2026 - July 1, 2027 | 102 |

**Important sourcing warning.** The Cigna document is not a facet injection coverage policy. It decides when moderate sedation or monitored anesthesia care is covered during pain procedures, and facet joint injection appears only as one of the procedures it may accompany. Cigna's facet criteria are in separate policies the document names under Related Coverage Resources ("Facet Joint Injections/Medial Branch Blocks" and "Interventional Pain Management Guidelines") and are not in the library. The Cigna column below therefore covers sedation only. Carelon and Evolent PDFs are also filed under epidural-injections; their profiles for that treatment were written in a separate pass, so the facet profiles are saved with a `--facet-joint-injections` suffix.

## What it is

Facet (zygapophyseal) joints are the small paired joints at the back of the spine. They can be a source of chronic neck, mid-back and low-back pain. A facet injection puts anesthetic, usually with steroid, into the joint (intra-articular) or around the tiny medial branch nerve that supplies it (a medial branch block, MBB), under X-ray (fluoroscopy) or CT. Because exam and MRI cannot reliably prove that a facet joint is the pain source, the procedure is used first to diagnose (does a numbing block abolish the pain?) and then, in some policies, to treat. Payers gate it on chronic axial pain of a certain intensity or functional loss, a defined period of failed conservative care, absence of nerve-root or other pathology, imaging guidance, and tight frequency limits per spinal region. Many payers prefer radiofrequency ablation after positive blocks and restrict therapeutic joint injections.

## Agreement and disagreement

**Aligned**
- Chronic pain of at least 3 months; predominantly axial, not radicular (synovial cyst is the one carve-out in all three); fracture, tumor and infection absent; imaging guidance required.
- A diagnostic step comes before treatment; frequency capped per spinal region per rolling 12 months (about 4 therapeutic-type procedures).
- Evidence of benefit (a percentage of pain relief for a stated period) must be in the chart for repeats.

**Where Florida Blue is stricter**
- Pain threshold: FL >= 6/10 (or functional deficit); Carelon needs only >= 3/10 with 2 ADLs lost. Evolent matches FL (>= 6) but also allows functional disability.
- Conservative care duration: FL needs 3 months of documented failure; Carelon needs 6 weeks, Evolent 6 weeks.
- Diagnostic response for therapeutic injections: FL requires >= 80% relief from the diagnostic injection; Evolent requires >= 50%. Carelon also uses 80% for MBB.
- FL's relief after a prior therapeutic injection: 3 months; Evolent only 2.
- FL excludes ultrasound-guided injections as investigational; Evolent accepts them when CT/fluoro are contraindicated.
- FL asks for pain assessment at baseline and after each injection (a series in the chart).

**Where Florida Blue is looser**
- Conservative care has no required components: FL says "noninvasive conservative care management (as tolerated)". Carelon requires physical therapy plus a second strategy; Evolent requires one active plus one inactive modality with HEP documentation rules. A chart with only NSAIDs could satisfy FL but not the others.
- FL allows therapeutic intra-articular injection as a routine second step after a successful diagnostic block; Carelon says it is "generally considered not medically necessary" except in four listed circumstances and prefers RF neurotomy after dual MBB.
- FL allows diagnostic intra-articular injection; Carelon excludes it ("Diagnostic intraarticular facet joint injection").
- FL has no restriction on steroid in diagnostic blocks; Carelon excludes "Therapeutic medial branch block (use of corticosteroid with diagnostic medial branch block)".
- FL covers thoracic and sacral regions; Carelon excludes thoracic except two junctions and defines regions only as cervical or lumbar; Evolent excludes sacral lateral branch blocks.
- FL does not require imaging for the cyst exception or documented physical exam findings; Carelon requires MRI/CT within 12 months and exam consistent with facet origin.
- FL does not need documentation of a patient commitment or HEP; Evolent's shared decision-making statement is in Background.
- FL has no stated two-year new-episode reset (Evolent has).

**Unique to one payer**
- FL: ICD-10 support list, including shoulder pain codes M25.511-M25.572; LOINC documentation table; explicit "80%" diagnostic relief gate for therapeutic injections; Florida step-therapy exemption.
- Carelon: independent radiologist read ("the radiology report will supersede"); redline format; spondylolisthesis grade >= 3 and pars defect exclusions; prior-fusion exclusion; two levels per session; RFN rules in the same section; peer-to-peer fallback; "Repeat Diagnostic/Therapeutic Intervention" general framework.
- Evolent: two-year new-episode reset; right/left same-level injections count as one procedure within a month; separate authorization per date of service; 7-day spacing across regions; occipital nerve blocks and hardware injections excluded; "medication or contrast agent allergy" relative contraindication.
- Cigna 0551: sedation criteria.

## Structure

- **Layout.** FL: Position Statement of three short blocks (general criteria, diagnostic, therapeutic) plus the ultrasound sentence. Carelon: a doc-wide General Clinical Guideline, then per-procedure chapters each with Description, Clinical Indications (General Information, Patient Requirements, Procedural Requirements, then procedure-specific), Exclusions, References, Codes; the facet chapter handles four procedures. Evolent: eight separate guidelines stitched together; each has Statement, Indications, Exclusions, Contraindications, Coding, Background (with the definitions used by the indications), evidence, history, references. Cigna: two criteria lists then 25 pages of code tables.
- **Logic shape.** FL: all-of general criteria AND (diagnostic all-of OR therapeutic all-of), nested depth 3. Carelon: all-of Patient Requirements, then per-procedure rules; therapeutic IA is a pathway (4 alternative exceptions), one with its own all-of; general requirements are overridden by specific ones. Evolent: all-of pain criteria with a nested sub-OR for pain character and three-way failure definition, plus a phase model for repeats. Cigna: any-of (immobility procedure; or listed procedure AND risk factor).
- **Where criteria live.** Evolent and Carelon define terms (conservative treatment, failure, severity) away from the criteria that use them (Background; General Information). FL's pain assessment requirement is in a footnote.
- **Delegation.** FL: MA to LCD L33930; separate RF policy. Carelon: health plan may substitute its own policy; peer-to-peer. Evolent: lines of business list; legislative text moved to an external document. Cigna: companion policy for the injection itself.
- **Codes.** All three facet documents list 64490-64495 plus 0213T-0218T; none maps codes to the diagnostic/therapeutic distinction. Only FL lists ICD-10. Carelon defines regions by code range ("cervical (CPT codes 64490, 64491, 64492) or lumbar (CPT codes 64493, 64494, 64495)").
- **Time and count.** Windows are everywhere: 3 months, 6 weeks, 1 or 2 weeks apart, 2-3 months of relief, rolling 12 months, 2 years. Counters are per spinal region, per level, per session.

## Problems for an automated checker

1. **Prior-injection history is required but a chart for the current request may not include it.** FL therapeutic criteria: "Diagnostic injection provided at least 80% pain relief" and "Subsequent therapeutic injections ... at least 50% pain relief for at least three (3) months from the prior therapeutic injection". A first therapeutic injection has no "subsequent" or "prior therapeutic injection": the criterion is vacuous or unsatisfiable, and the document does not say which.
2. **Diagnostic vs therapeutic is not in the code.** The same CPT codes 64490-64495 cover both; the checker must decide the mode from the order text (steroid or local anesthetic only, repeat or first). Carelon even makes the mode depend on the agent: diagnostic MBB must be local anesthetic only.
3. **Mixed OR/AND on one line.** FL: "Pain of >= 6 on a scale of 0 to 10, OR functional deficit, AND*" and the asterisk footnote "Pain assessment must be performed and documented at baseline, and after each injection" which is a criterion living in a footnote. Evolent has the same "functional disability or average pain level of >= 6", which turns a numeric gate into an almost always satisfied OR because "functional disability" is undefined.
4. **Hidden conflicts inside one document.** Carelon: "a maximum of four (4) diagnostic joint sessions ... per rolling 12 months" and "A maximum of only 2 diagnostic sessions per level ... in a given year"; both can hold only with a specific distribution. Evolent: "In the diagnostic phase, a maximum of 2 procedures may be performed" and "A maximum of 4 facet injections may be performed in a 12-month period per spinal region" do not say whether diagnostic injections count toward the 4.
5. **Condition list with embedded sub-rules.** Carelon's four exceptions for therapeutic IA are numbered 1-4, but an unnumbered "For the exceptions listed above only:" list with repeat rules sits between items 3 and 4, so it is unclear whether those limits apply to cyst compression (item 4), which has its own repeat rule ("Cyst aspiration/rupture may be repeated once within the 12-month period").
6. **Redline artifacts (Carelon).** The PDF is a redline with insertions underlined and deletions struck through. pdftotext keeps both with no marking: "Effective Date: 04/14/2024 04/09/2023", "© 20243", "rootneural elements". Any number in the facet section could be a deleted old value. The text alone cannot say which; the PDF must be inspected visually, or the clean version obtained.
7. **Terms the chart cannot easily supply.** FL: "functional deficit", "noninvasive conservative care management (as tolerated)", "suggestive of facet joint origin", "significant spinal deformity", "duration of relief being consistent with the agent used" (needs drug and expected duration). Carelon: "Physical exam findings which are consistent with the facet joint", "older individuals on anticoagulation". Evolent: "meaningful improvement", "significant documented functional improvement", "unusual circumstances, such as a recurrent injury".
8. **Relief percentages come from patient-reported or procedural notes.** An 80% relief figure is usually in a post-procedure pain diary or note. The checker needs a dated pain score before and after, which a chart summary may lack.
9. **Criteria a chart cannot show.** "Absence of prior surgical fusion at the proposed level" (needs level mapping); "Absence of nonfacet pathology" (absence is never charted, silence is INSUFFICIENT); "More invasive forms of therapy are being considered" (an intent statement); "shared decision-making between patient and physician must occur" (Evolent); injection counts across other facilities.
10. **Spinal region logic.** FL limits one spinal region per session with four regions including sacral; Carelon limits to cervical/lumbar by code, with thoracic exceptions by level (C7-T1, T12-L1); Evolent excludes S1-S3 lateral branch blocks. A request for "L5-S1" or "sacral" depends on which definition applies.
11. **Footnote definitions after use (Evolent).** "Conservative treatment*" and "HEP**" are defined in Background pages after the criteria. A parser that stops at the Indications section will treat them as undefined. Citation numbers in headings, "Facet Joint Pain (1)" and "Repeat Injections (1,6)", look like values.
12. **Bundled PDF (Evolent).** CG 1750, 1751, 1753, 1754 and others share near-identical headings and the same Special Note text; extracting the wrong guideline's thresholds is easy (CG 1753 diagnostic success is 50%; CG 1754 uses 70% and 4 months).
13. **Wrong-document hazard (Cigna).** The Cigna file names facet joint injection in criteria lists (sedation), so a naive extractor may report "Cigna covers facet injection when severe anxiety is present". The document says nothing about necessity of the injection.
14. **ICD-10 support list at FL** includes M25.511-M25.572 (shoulder pain), which are not spinal facet diagnoses; the list "support[s] medical necessity" with no mapping to criteria. Not a rule a checker should enforce literally.
15. **Soft rules.** Carelon's "In general, repeated therapeutic intervention ... is considered appropriate when the prior intervention proved effective", "most likely require a peer-to-peer conversation", and "generally considered not medically necessary" are not binary.
16. **Counts within rolling windows** need claims history, not chart notes.

## Test cases

Facts are invented; each case yields different outcomes by payer, with criteria cited. Cigna cannot be judged here (document governs sedation only).

1. **Therapeutic intra-articular steroid injection after one good diagnostic block: FL and Evolent approve, Carelon does not.**
   57-year-old with 7 months of axial low back pain worse with extension, average pain 7/10, no radiculopathy, MRI shows mild facet arthropathy and no fracture or tumor. 12 weeks of physical therapy plus NSAIDs without relief. A single diagnostic lumbar MBB 3 weeks ago gave 85% relief for the expected 4 hours. Requests a fluoroscopic intra-articular lumbar facet steroid injection (64493). No inflammatory arthritis, no pacemaker, no anticoagulants, no plan to avoid denervation.
   - FL: general criteria met (axial, pain >= 6, 3 months with documented failure of conservative care, no nonfacet pathology); therapeutic criteria met because the "Diagnostic injection provided at least 80% pain relief" and fluoroscopy is used. Approve (first therapeutic injection; "subsequent" rule not yet in play).
   - Evolent: therapeutic phase follows a "successful initial treatment phase that provided at least 50% relief"; pain >= 6 persists; active conservative care documented. Approve.
   - Carelon: "Therapeutic intraarticular facet injections are generally considered not medically necessary for facetogenic axial low back pain"; none of exceptions 1-4 applies (no inflammatory arthropathy, denervation not contraindicated, no RFN risk, no cyst); also only a single MBB was done where "Dual medial branch blocks ... are necessary". Not medically necessary; radiofrequency neurotomy after a second MBB is the path.

2. **Medications only: FL approves, Carelon and Evolent do not.**
   63-year-old with 5 months of axial neck pain, average 7/10, worse with rotation, no neurological deficit, MRI without fracture or tumor. Treated with naproxen and cyclobenzaprine for 3 months with no improvement; no physical therapy, chiropractic or home exercise program. Requests a fluoroscopic cervical medial branch block (64490).
   - FL: pain >= 6, 3 months, "documented failure to respond to noninvasive conservative care management". The text names no components, so medications can qualify. Approve (initial diagnostic injection).
   - Carelon: "Conservative management should include ... physical therapy AND at least one complementary" strategy. Medications are the complementary arm only; PT absent and no documented exception (intractable pain). Patient Requirements not met.
   - Evolent: needs "at least one (1) active and one (1) inactive component" for 6 weeks in the last 6 months. Medications are inactive; no active component. Not met.

3. **Mid-back (thoracic) medial branch block: FL and Evolent approve, Carelon does not.**
   49-year-old with 6 months of thoracic back pain at T6-T8, axial, average 6/10, 10 weeks of PT and NSAIDs without relief, no red flags. Requests diagnostic fluoroscopic thoracic MBB (64490).
   - FL: thoracic is one of the listed regions (cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacral) and general criteria are met. Approve.
   - Evolent: no thoracic exclusion in CG 1753; criteria met. Approve.
   - Carelon: exclusions include "Diagnostic or therapeutic intraarticular injection, medial branch block, or RFA in the thoracic region with the exception of C7-T1 and T12-L1". T6-T8 is excluded. Not medically necessary.

4. **Ultrasound-guided injection when fluoroscopy is not possible (bonus): Evolent only.**
   A patient with BMI 52 who cannot be positioned for fluoroscopy requests ultrasound-guided lumbar facet injection (0216T). FL: "experimental or investigational". Carelon: excluded ("Any facet joint interventions performed under ultrasound guidance"). Evolent: ultrasound "can be an effective alternative if CT or fluoroscopy guided techniques are contraindicated", and a note says poor visualization at high BMI may limit it; a reviewer decision.

## Notes for the checker

- The Florida Blue facet guideline is a good second test of the tree: simple shape, but nearly every leaf depends on injection history and dated pain scores. The common outcome will be INSUFFICIENT rather than NOT_MET for first-time requests, because "subsequent" and "prior" criteria cannot be evaluated.
- For a corpus on facet injection, Cigna's own facet policy needs to be added to the library; 0551 is the wrong document.
- Get a clean (non-redline) Carelon copy before extracting numbers from it.
