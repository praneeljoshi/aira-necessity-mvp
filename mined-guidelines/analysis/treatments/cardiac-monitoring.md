# Cardiac monitoring: cross-payer analysis

Documents profiled (all in `sourcing/analysis/profiles/`): Florida Blue 01-93000-05 (13 pp), Blue Shield of California 2.02.08 (70 pp), Cigna 0547 (35 pp), Excellus 2.01.03 (16 pp), Curative MM-PNP-009 (5 pp).

## What it is

Ambulatory cardiac monitoring records heart rhythm outside the hospital to catch arrhythmias that a clinic ECG misses. The ladder runs from Holter (24-48 h, not covered by these policies) to external event monitors and long-wear patches (more than 48 h, up to 30 days), mobile cardiac outpatient telemetry (MCOT, patch or device that streams events to an attended center in real time), and the implantable loop recorder (ILR, a subcutaneous device that records for up to about 3 years). Payers gate each rung on (1) the indication (infrequent syncope/palpitations, cryptogenic stroke, post-ablation AF), (2) whether a cheaper rung already failed to give a diagnosis, and (3) device-specific limits (MCOT is the most restricted; apps, smartwatches and ST-segment monitoring are almost universally investigational or a "convenience item").

## Agreement and disagreement

**Where payers align**
- Everyone treats apps, smartwatches and ST-segment or ischemia monitoring as non-covered (investigational or convenience).
- Escalation ladder: Holter or external monitor first, then MCOT or ILR when the cheaper test failed.
- Cryptogenic stroke and post-ablation AF are covered indications in all five (Florida Blue, BSCA, Cigna, Excellus, Curative) for some device.
- Florida Blue and BSCA share nearly word-for-word text (both derive from the BCBSA reference policy).

**Where they differ**
- MCOT: Excellus denies it entirely; Cigna and Curative cover it with broad indication lists; BSCA requires an unusual "essential for patient safety" documentation; Florida Blue requires a prior non-diagnostic external trial within 60 days.
- Frequency thresholds: Florida Blue/BSCA "every 48 hours"; Curative "daily" (external) and "monthly" (ILR); Cigna none (except pediatric >30 days); Excellus none.
- Duration of the prior failed monitoring before ILR: Cigna 14 days, Excellus 28 days, Curative 30 days; Florida Blue and BSCA no duration, only "unsuccessful".
- Cigna alone uses a risk score (CHA2DS2-VASc >=2) and covers hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, AF cardiomyopathy and AF in acute illness; has pediatric ILR criteria.
- Curative alone stratifies ILR by heart failure, prior MI or ECG abnormalities.
- Excellus alone defines a "thorough stroke evaluation" and requires plan-of-care documentation for ILR.
- Repeat-study rules: Excellus and Curative (1 year), Cigna (ILR replacement only); Florida Blue and BSCA none.

**Unique to one payer**: Florida Blue's MCOT exclusion of "life-threatening arrhythmias" and its CardioMEMS/CIG investigational statements; Cigna's pediatric section and ICD "not covered" tables; Excellus's convenience-item and ILR replacement rules; Curative's high-risk syncope list.

**Florida Blue vs the others**
- Stricter: MCOT requires a non-diagnostic external trial within 60 days AND a narrow second condition, whereas Cigna and Curative accept many indications (Cigna still requires non-diagnostic external monitoring, but with no time window). Florida Blue explicitly denies MCOT for "monitoring members with life-threatening arrhythmias" and "ongoing management after diagnosis". Florida Blue's external-monitor indications are fewer than Cigna's (no cardiomyopathy, CTI flutter, or AF in acute illness; no drug-response indications as in Excellus/Curative).
- Looser: The ILR has no external-monitor prerequisite in the second indication ("long-term monitoring for atrial fibrillation or possible atrial fibrillation"), whereas Cigna requires 14 days of inconclusive monitoring, Excellus 28 days, and Curative 30 days. No plan-of-care documentation (Excellus requires it). No repeat-study or replacement limits. No device-brand lists. More permissive than Excellus on MCOT (Excellus denies all).
- Looser than BSCA on MCOT: no "real-time monitoring essential for patient safety" requirement.
- Stricter than BSCA on post-ablation external monitors: Florida Blue says "discontinuation of systemic anticoagulation is being considered"; BSCA drops the "considered" language ("To guide discontinuation").

## Structure

- **Layout**: Florida Blue is a one-page Position Statement of bullets by device type, followed by code tables. BSCA is a 70-page document with criteria on pp.1-2, 50+ pages of evidence, and an Appendix A redline repeating old and new criteria. Cigna is a 3-page coverage policy and heavy per-code ICD tables. Excellus has Roman-numbered statements plus Policy Guidelines that carry definitions. Curative is a corporate UM template (purpose, scope, enforcement) with a 2.5-page policy section.
- **Logic shape**: All are pathways-by-device. Within a device the indications are any-of ("ANY of the following" at Cigna, "ANY of the following indications" at Excellus). Florida Blue's MCOT is the only all-of block with a nested "ONE of". Curative's ILR block has a nested all-of with trailing "or" at inconsistent indents. Maximum depth 3.
- **Criteria location**: Excellus splits a definition (thorough stroke evaluation) and the repeat rule into Policy Guidelines. Cigna puts CHA2DS2-VASc below the statement as a footnote. Curative's "see appendix" points to a list at Section 8.
- **Delegation**: Florida Blue sends Medicare Advantage to NCDs/LCD; FEP/SAO to their own guidelines. Cigna lists Medicare determinations only for reference. None of the five delegates to MCG or InterQual here.
- **Codes**: Florida Blue and Cigna map ICD-10 lists to CPT groups; Excellus tags codes with (NMN)/(E/I); BSCA lists as "general reference"; Curative lists none. Florida Blue's table has a duplicate description for 93243 (looks like a typo).

## Problems for an automated checker

- **Undefined "non-diagnostic"**: Florida Blue, BSCA, Cigna and Curative all use "non-diagnostic" or "inconclusive" for the prior monitor. A chart saying "no events captured, normal rhythm" is ambiguous. BSCA adds "and/or ... nondiagnostic or contraindicated".
- **"negative standard work-up"**: Florida Blue: "Members with cryptogenic stroke who have a negative standard work-up for atrial fibrillation including a 24-hour Holter monitor". Only Holter is specified; the checker must decide what else counts.
- **Future intent**: "in whom discontinuation of systemic anticoagulation is being considered" (Florida Blue); "when the results will directly impact clinical decision-making" (Cigna); "will significantly change the plan of care" (Excellus); "will lead to immediate clinical changes" (BSCA). These are predictions, rarely in a chart; INSUFFICIENT is the likely verdict.
- **Open-ended thresholds**: Cigna "deemed to be at high thromboembolic risk (e.g., CHA2DS2-VASc* score >=2)" makes the score an example, not a requirement. "severe palpitations", "significant bradyarrhythmia", "clinically significant presyncope" have no definition.
- **And/or hazards**: Florida Blue's external-monitor bullets end with "; OR" only after bullet 2. Cigna's "continues to meet ALL of the above criteria" for ILR replacement is impossible against an any-of list. Curative: "when either criteria in 1 or 2 is met" but only one numbered bullet exists, and its ILR block mixes "and" and "or" at one indent. Excellus Guideline III has stray text "a, or".
- **Extraction**: BSCA's Appendix A redline interleaves old and new text; the old version declared MCOT investigational, so a naive parse can read superseded criteria. Cigna's 14 pages of ICD tables repeat headers. Excellus's code cells embed (NMN) and (E/I) flags.
- **Chart cannot show**: whether an earlier monitor was "unsuccessful" if it was done elsewhere; "within the last 60 days" requires dated reports; 'cryptogenic' is a diagnosis of exclusion; "stroke center certification" is not in the chart; CHA2DS2-VASc must be computed from several fields.
- **Version conflict**: BSCA metadata says effective 07/01/2023 while the document says August 1, 2026.

## Test cases

1. **58-year-old, 2 episodes of syncope in 6 months, normal 7-day patch (non-diagnostic) 3 weeks ago, no stroke.** Florida Blue MCOT: the patch was within 60 days and non-diagnostic, but the member lacks a cryptogenic stroke history; "recurrent, unexplained episodes of syncope or presyncope of suspected arrhythmic etiology" is the other arm, so MET if the physician documents arrhythmic suspicion. Cigna approves MCOT too (syncope + non-diagnostic external). Excellus denies (MCOT not medically necessary). BSCA denies unless documentation shows real-time monitoring "essential for patient safety".
2. **70-year-old with paroxysmal AF found during hospitalization for hip surgery, CHA2DS2-VASc 4, no symptoms.** Cigna approves external monitoring under "AF in the setting of acute medical illness or surgery" (score >=2). Florida Blue denies: asymptomatic patients with risk factors are investigational and there is no acute-illness indication. BSCA also investigational.
3. **45-year-old with infrequent palpitations once every 3 weeks, no prior monitor tried, requesting an ILR.** Florida Blue's second ILR indication ("long-term monitoring for atrial fibrillation or possible atrial fibrillation") has no external-monitor prerequisite, so it may approve if AF is suspected; its first indication needs a failed external trial. Cigna (>=14 days inconclusive monitoring) and Excellus (failed 28-day external monitor, or contraindicated) deny. Curative is a split decision: the low-risk arm fails (symptoms are more often than monthly), but its second pathway (arrhythmia suspected, symptoms "less frequently than daily" so Holter is unlikely to diagnose) could be read as met. This is the case where Florida Blue is looser than the others.
