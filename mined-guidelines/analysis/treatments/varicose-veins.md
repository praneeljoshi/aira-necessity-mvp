# Varicose veins / venous insufficiency: four payer documents compared

Documents (all in `sourcing/library/varicose-veins/`, profiles in `sourcing/analysis/profiles/`):

| Short name | Document | Version | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Florida Blue (FL)** | BCBSFL 02-33000-31, Treatments for Varicose Veins/Venous Insufficiency | Revised 01/01/26, reviewed 07/24/25 | 14 |
| **BCBSRI** | Blue Cross & Blue Shield of Rhode Island, Varicose Vein Treatment (Medical Coverage Policy) | Effective 05/01/2019, updated 07/07/2021 | 10 |
| **UHC** | UnitedHealthcare Commercial/Exchange 2026T0447SS, Surgical and Ablative Procedures for Venous Insufficiency and Varicose Veins | Effective 07/01/2026 | 16 |
| **UHC-MA** | UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage, Varicose Veins Treatment and Other Vein Embolization Procedures | PDF text says MMP099.11, effective 09/01/2026 (library metadata says MMP099.07 / 2024; trust the PDF) | 4 |

The headline: only Florida Blue and BCBSRI contain criteria you can read. UHC (both) is a pointer document: criteria live in InterQual, which is not in the file. Florida Blue is the only one of the four that is a fully self-contained, vein-by-vein criteria set with measurable thresholds.

## What it is

Varicose veins and chronic venous insufficiency come from leaky valves that let blood flow backward (reflux) in the leg's superficial veins (great, small and accessory saphenous veins, their tributaries) or in perforator veins that connect to the deep system. Treatments either remove the vein (ligation and stripping, phlebectomy), close it by heat (radiofrequency or laser ablation), by glue (cyanoacrylate) or by chemistry (foam or liquid sclerotherapy, mechanochemical ablation), or tie off perforators. Payers gate it on one question: is this treating a symptomatic or complicated disease (ulcer, bleeding, phlebitis, symptoms that disrupt life and did not respond to compression), or is it cosmetic? They back that with a duplex ultrasound showing reflux, a minimum vein size, and a trial of compression or conservative care. They also draw lines around which technique is proven (MOCA, cryoablation and similar are almost universally "unproven"), and sequence rules for which vein must be treated first.

## Agreement and disagreement

**Where payers align**
- Telangiectasia / spider veins are cosmetic everywhere. 36468 is non-covered at FL, BCBSRI and UHC.
- MOCA (ClariVein) is not covered by FL (investigational), BCBSRI (not medically necessary) and UHC (unproven). UHC-MA defers to LCDs.
- Concurrent perforator ablation with superficial surgery: FL and BCBSRI both say not medically necessary.
- The perforator block is nearly identical in FL and BCBSRI (reflux; superficial veins eliminated; ulcers unresolved after combined treatment plus compression for at least 3 months; not secondary to DVT). Their wording is close enough that both likely draw on the same BCBSA evidence text (FL cites BCBSA 7.01.124; RI's Background reads the same), but neither document says so.
- Both Blues tie at least one indication to symptoms that "interfere" with activities of daily living.
- Both Blues say "significant" without defining it.

**Where Florida Blue is stricter**
- Conservative therapy length: 3 months of compression (FL) vs 6 weeks of conservative therapy (BCBSRI). FL's compression trial is a hard requirement of the symptom indication; the RI list includes items like exercise and weight reduction that need no compression.
- Vein size: FL needs varicosities >= 3 mm for every request. BCBSRI sets a size rule only for sclerotherapy (> 2 and < 6 mm) and echosclerotherapy, none for ablation. Veins between 2 and 3 mm can qualify for sclerotherapy at BCBSRI but not at FL.
- CEAP: FL needs class C2 or greater; BCBSRI never asks for a CEAP class.
- Sclerotherapy scope: FL limits to microfoam for truncal veins and calls liquid sclerotherapy of the great/small/accessory saphenous veins investigational; BCBSRI covers liquid sclerotherapy under size rules.
- Isolated tributary sclerotherapy (no prior or concurrent saphenous treatment) is investigational at FL; BCBSRI allows sclerotherapy when the saphenous veins are competent, removed, ablated or ablation planned (so an untreated competent saphenous vein with tributary disease can qualify at BCBSRI but not at FL).
- Ultrasound guidance for tributary sclerotherapy is declared not medically necessary at FL (BCBSRI covers duplex-guided echosclerotherapy S2202 for perforators).
- Ablation of tributaries by RF/laser is investigational at FL.
- FL perforator treatment is explicitly for ulcers only and needs >= 3 mm.
- Accessory vein: FL imposes a separate block with a 3-month wait after saphenous elimination; BCBSRI does not.

**Where Florida Blue is looser**
- Three of FL's four indications (ulcer, recurrent superficial thrombophlebitis, bleeding) carry no conservative-therapy requirement. BCBSRI requires 6 weeks of conservative therapy and a duplex ultrasound before any indication, including an ulcer or bleeding.
- FL symptom indication 4 accepts "pain, swelling, itching, burning, or other symptoms" (any one). BCBSRI's matching item needs pain AND edema both.
- FL has no session cap on sclerotherapy; UHC makes a fourth session per leg in a rolling year cosmetic, and BCBSRI sends it to review.
- FL covers cyanoacrylate (36482, 36483) for saphenous veins explicitly, while the revision history shows it was "investigational" in 2020; this is a recent loosening. BCBSRI lists cyanoacrylate codes as medically necessary and UHC leaves it to InterQual.
- FL does not require a duplex by name.
- FL does not require documentation of compression intolerance (the Florida step-therapy exemption statute in Program Exceptions may serve this).

**Unique to one payer**
- FL: CEAP C2 floor; accessory-vein block including the "discharged directly into the common femoral vein" rule; once-per-leg billing notes; LOINC documentation table with six-month recency; explicit ICD-10 range; state step-therapy exemption (Florida law 627.42393).
- BCBSRI: stasis dermatitis and refractory dependent edema as indications; sclerotherapy size window (> 2 and < 6 mm); perforator > 3.5 mm for echosclerotherapy; prior authorization via web tool; MA criteria limited to three codes.
- UHC: reflux-time definitions (0.5 s, 1 s) and axial reflux length (5 cm); cosmetic sclerotherapy cap by session and year; VenoValve (porcine valve) unproven; the "Individual Exchange" line of business; recurrent/residual perforator ablation unproven.
- UHC-MA: jurisdiction lookup (MAC by state) as the entire policy.

## Structure

- **Layout.** FL: one Position Statement of six headed blocks (Great or Small Saphenous; Accessory Saphenous; Symptomatic Varicose Tributaries; Perforator Veins; Telangiectasia; Other), then a CEAP footnote, then coding. BCBSRI: Overview, then Medical Criteria written twice (Medicare Advantage, Commercial), Prior Authorization, Policy Statement, long Background, Coverage, Coding. UHC: contents page, Coverage Rationale of a few lines, Definitions, Codes, long narrative and evidence. UHC-MA: four pages, mostly tables.
- **Logic shape.** FL: pathways by vein type; each is an all-of whose last member is a 1-of-4 indication list; indication 4 is itself an all-of of three items. Depth 4. This is the cleanest tree of the four. BCBSRI: all-of(general gate, method gate) where the general gate is all-of(duplex, 6 weeks conservative, 1-of-6). UHC and UHC-MA: delegation, not logic.
- **Where criteria live.** FL: Position Statement only, except documentation expectations (LOINC paragraph under Billing/Coding) and the CEAP key (footnote). BCBSRI: Medical Criteria plus extra rules in Policy Statement and Coding. UHC: thresholds hidden in Definitions and Coding Clarifications.
- **Delegation.** UHC to InterQual (two products named; one is a "Client Defined ... (Custom) - UHG" subset), UHC-MA to MAC LCDs, InterQual and back to the commercial policy; BCBSRI-MA to CMS via an online tool; FL-MA to First Coast L38720/A58250 (reviewed, not incorporated).
- **Codes.** FL lists CPT with three tags (36468 non-covered; 36473, 36474, 0524T investigational), HCPCS S2202, ICD-10 ranges. Codes are not mapped to blocks. 37785 and S2202 appear with no position statement of their own. BCBSRI lists more codes under "medically necessary when the medical criteria above are met" but codes are not mapped to criteria either (and 37500 is a code deleted in 2026). UHC lists codes "for reference purposes only" and states "Listing of a code in this policy does not imply that the service ... is a covered or non-covered health service."
- **Sequencing.** FL has three prior-procedure dependencies: accessory needs saphenous eliminated 3 months earlier; tributary treatment needs saphenous treated concurrently or earlier; perforator needs all superficial veins eliminated plus a 3-month ulcer trial. A single-visit chart rarely states these.

## Problems for an automated checker

**Florida Blue specifically** (the project's anchor guideline)

1. **Nested logic flattened by bullets.** Indication 4 is `symptoms AND ADL interference AND 3 months failed compression` and sits inside a 1-of-4 list: "Persistent pain, swelling, itching, burning, or other symptoms are associated with saphenous reflux, AND the symptoms significantly interfere with activities of daily living, AND conservative management including compression therapy for at least 3 months has not improved the symptoms." Indications 1-3 never require conservative care, so a flat all-of that includes the compression leaf wrongly fails an ulcer patient. The hand-built tree in this repo models it correctly; a generated tree must too.
2. **"demonstrated saphenous reflux".** No definition of what demonstrates it. The chart must carry a duplex report; the policy does not name duplex, reflux seconds, or the segment. UHC's definitions (0.5 s) are not FL's.
3. **"varicosities are at least 3 millimeters in size".** Measured where? On duplex (vein diameter, often 5-9 mm for a refluxing GSV) or visible varicosities on exam? Because the leaf sits at the same level as the reflux criterion, a chart that says "GSV 6.2 mm" likely satisfies it, but a chart with a 2.5 mm tributary measurement alone will fail. The text says "varicosities", not "saphenous vein".
4. **"significantly interfere with activities of daily living".** "Significantly" is undefined; no scale. The checker needs a quote that describes functional limitation (work, standing, walking). Charts often state "symptomatic" without ADLs.
5. **"has not improved the symptoms".** No improvement threshold. A chart that says "partial relief with stockings" is ambiguous.
6. **Compression start and end dates.** The checker has to establish "at least 3 months" from dated notes. A chart that says "wearing compression stockings" without a start date is INSUFFICIENT; this is the exact miss in the repo's Linda Park sample (compression start date).
7. **"including compression therapy".** "including" makes compression one example, so can elevation or exercise for 3 months substitute? The Description defines conservative treatment as "elevation of the extremities, graded compression, and wound care when indicated"; the block says "including compression". Safest reading: compression is required. This is a judgment call to record.
8. **CEAP class not stated in the chart.** The footnote key has C2, C2r, C3 ... C6r. Charts often describe findings without a class. The checker must map findings to a class (varicose veins = C2, edema = C3, pigmentation = C4a, healed ulcer = C5, active ulcer = C6); "or greater" over letters with sub-suffixes (C4a/b/c, C2r, C6r) is a partial order the text does not spell out. The "S"/"A" suffix (symptomatic/asymptomatic) in the footnote is easy to misread as a criterion.
9. **Prior-procedure leaves.** "previously eliminated (at least 3 months)" and "have been previously eliminated" need procedure dates and outcome confirmation (was the vein eliminated, or just treated?). Many charts say "s/p EVLA" without a follow-up duplex.
10. **"isolated".** "Incompetence of the accessory saphenous vein is isolated" needs a duplex statement that no other saphenous reflux exists.
11. **Exclusions written as sentences, not rules.** "Ligation or ablation of incompetent perforator veins performed concurrently with superficial venous surgery does not meet the definition of medical necessity" depends on the other procedures on the same order, not the chart. "Sclerotherapy of isolated tributary veins without prior or concurrent treatment of saphenous veins" is another cross-procedure rule.
12. **Procedure-to-block mapping is by prose.** One CPT can fall under different blocks depending on the target vein (e.g. 37735 "radical excision of ulcer ... interruption of communicating veins"; 36470/36471 for tributaries vs perforators vs truncal veins). The document gives no CPT-to-block table. The hand-built tree assigns CPTs to blocks by judgment.
13. **Codes with no criteria.** S2202 (echosclerotherapy), 37785 (cluster excision) appear in the code table only. A checker has to decide they ride on the tributary rule.
14. **Dead and contradictory text.** The revision history (01/15/20) says "investigational position for cyanoacrylate adhesive maintained" while the current position covers cyanoacrylate. A parser reading the whole PDF will find both. Only the Position Statement is current.
15. **Documentation expectation not in the criteria.** The LOINC paragraph says "failure of at least 3 consecutive months of medical management" ("consecutive" is not in the Position Statement) and sets a six-month recency window for records. A checker that only reads the Position Statement misses these.
16. **Program exceptions change who is checked.** FEP and SAO "follow ... guidelines" (not this one); Medicare Advantage uses First Coast LCD L38720. Applicability must be decided before judging; the repo already routes MA away.
17. **ICD-10 range.** "I83.001 – I83.899" spans hundreds of codes, with en-dash and spacing that break naive parsing. Code-level checks are not mapped to specific criteria.

**Across the four documents**
- Two of four contain no criteria. An extractor asked for a tree should return "delegated" and name the external document; inventing leaves from definitions (UHC's 3 mm and 0.5 s) would be wrong, since no statement requires them.
- BCBSRI duplicates the criteria for two lines of business with small differences, and uses `and/or` strings ("greater saphenous and/or lesser saphenous and/or accessory saphenous vein is competent; or removed or ablated") that cannot be read as plain all/any.
- BCBSRI's general gate (duplex + 6 weeks + 1-of-6) applies before the method gate; the Policy Statement says "medical criteria is met for both treatment and the method", a two-layer rule that exists only in prose.
- Document date and number conflicts: the UHC-MA PDF text says MMP099.11 / effective 9/1/2026 while the library metadata says MMP099.07 / 2024; a checker keyed on policy number could misidentify.
- Page furniture (running headers, 'Proprietary Information' footers, nav bars) interrupts lists across all four.

## Test cases

Facts are invented for illustration; each scenario shows a different outcome by payer, citing criteria. UHC cannot be judged from its document (criteria in InterQual), so these compare Florida Blue with BCBSRI and, where the text allows, UHC.

1. **Active ulcer, no compression trial yet: FL approves, BCBSRI does not.**
   62-year-old with an active venous ulcer (CEAP C6) above the left ankle for 5 weeks. Duplex: great saphenous vein reflux, GSV 6 mm. No compression trial yet. Requests RF ablation of the GSV (36475).
   - FL: saphenous block. Reflux demonstrated, C6 is >= C2, 6 mm >= 3 mm, and indication 1 (ulceration secondary to venous stasis) is met. Indications 1-3 carry no conservative-therapy condition, so the missing compression trial does not matter. Approve.
   - BCBSRI: general gate requires "remains symptomatic after 6 weeks of conservative therapy" before any indication; he has had none. Not met until six weeks of documented conservative therapy.

2. **Eight weeks of stockings for leg pain: BCBSRI approves, FL does not.**
   48-year-old with C3 disease, daily leg aching and swelling that keep her from standing through a work shift, GSV reflux on duplex (GSV 7 mm). She has worn 20-30 mmHg stockings for 8 weeks and elevates her legs, no improvement. Requests laser ablation (36478).
   - BCBSRI: duplex done, 6 weeks of conservative therapy satisfied (8 weeks of stockings and elevation), one of the six indications is met ("significant pain and significant edema that interferes with activities of daily living"), and method criterion is "saphenous vein is incompetent". Approve.
   - FL: indication 4 needs "compression therapy for at least 3 months"; 8 weeks falls short. No other indication applies. Not met; ask for 4 more weeks of documented compression.

3. **Small-vein sclerotherapy and a fourth session: three payers, three outcomes.**
   55-year-old whose GSV was ablated 9 months ago and who has had two liquid sclerotherapy sessions on the same leg for symptomatic tributaries (largest 2.5 mm, measured on duplex); a third and fourth are requested within the same rolling year (36471). Symptoms: aching, ADLs affected.
   - FL: tributary treatment by sclerotherapy is covered as a component after prior saphenous treatment; no session cap. But the policy requires varicosities of at least 3 mm in the saphenous/perforator blocks; the tributary block states no size. The 2.5 mm measurement is a gap a checker must flag (does the 3 mm apply to tributaries?). Likely approve; flag for review.
   - BCBSRI: sclerotherapy requires veins "> 2 mm and < 6 mm" (2.5 mm passes) and saphenous "removed, ablated". Requests above three sessions in 12 months "will be reviewed". The fourth session goes to review, the first three pass.
   - UHC: 36470/36471 "covered ... up to three sessions per leg within a year"; the fourth session is "considered cosmetic" and excluded. Deny the fourth.

4. **Untreated saphenous vein with tributary disease: BCBSRI allows sclerotherapy, FL does not.**
   Patient with symptomatic tributary varicosities, 4 mm, with a competent great saphenous vein on duplex, no prior treatment, symptoms for 4 months, 3 months of compression without relief. Requests liquid sclerotherapy (36470) of the tributaries only.
   - FL: "Sclerotherapy of isolated tributary veins without prior or concurrent treatment of saphenous veins" is investigational. Not covered.
   - BCBSRI: sclerotherapy needs veins > 2 and < 6 mm (4 mm passes) and "saphenous vein is competent" (it is). With duplex, 6 weeks conservative therapy, and a listed indication, approved.

## Notes for the project owner

- The Florida Blue document is the best-behaved of the set for a tree, but items 2, 3, 8, 9 and 12 above are where chart evidence will most often be INSUFFICIENT rather than NOT_MET.
- If the checker gets a chart for a UHC member, the right answer from the document is "criteria are in InterQual; not in this guideline".
- Library note: UHC-MA metadata (MMP099.07, 2024) disagrees with the PDF text (MMP099.11, 2026). Re-ingest from the PDF.
