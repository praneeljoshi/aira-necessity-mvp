# Sleep testing (HSAT, PSG, titration, MSLT/MWT)

Documents profiled: BCBSFL 01-95828-01 (anchor), UHC 2026T0334SS, BCBSM "Diagnosis of Sleep Disorders", Blue Shield CA 2.01.18, Cigna 0158.
**Warning on Cigna:** the library file `cigna-0158-...` is actually *Surgical Treatments for Obstructive Sleep Apnea*. It holds no HSAT/PSG/titration/MSLT criteria; it says those "are covered in the Sleep Disordered Breathing Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines" (not in the library). The Cigna column below is therefore mostly "not in this document". The correct Cigna document should be fetched before Cigna is used as a sleep-testing comparator.

## What it is
Sleep testing diagnoses sleep-disordered breathing (mainly obstructive sleep apnea, OSA) and other sleep disorders, and titrates positive airway pressure (PAP). Tests are an unattended home sleep apnea test (HSAT), attended in-lab polysomnography (PSG), a split-night study (diagnosis then PAP titration in one night), a full-night titration, and daytime MSLT/MWT for narcolepsy and wakefulness. Payers gate it on three things: (1) a symptom/risk picture suggesting OSA, (2) site of service: HSAT for uncomplicated adults, PSG when comorbidities or other sleep disorders could make HSAT inaccurate, and (3) repeat rules (prior non-diagnostic test, weight change, treatment failure). Pediatric testing runs on separate, looser rules.

## Agreement and disagreement
**Align:** adults get HSAT by default and PSG when HSAT is unreliable; the contraindication list is nearly the same set everywhere (moderate-severe lung disease, NYHA III/IV or EF <=40%, neuromuscular disease, opioids >3 months, OHS, CSA risk, parasomnia/narcolepsy/seizures). Pediatric HSAT is never covered (Florida Blue, Blue Shield CA, BCBSM). 10% weight change triggers repeat testing (Florida Blue, UHC, BCBSM). A PAP adherence definition of >=4 hours on 70% of nights over 30 days appears in Florida Blue and BCBSM. Insomnia, circadian disorders and screening of asymptomatic people are excluded broadly.

**Differ:**
- Entry symptom test: Florida Blue (A and B categories, or witnessed apnea) is stricter than UHC (just "suspected OSA", looser) and similar to BCBSM (2-of-5 or observed apnea). Florida Blue's category A includes plain "Fatigue", so it is easy to satisfy; BCBSM requires age >=18 and counts BMI >30 or neck circumference only inside "2 of 5".
- Florida Blue stricter: caps of 2 sleep studies per 12 months and 1 MSLT day (no other payer in the set states a cap); board-certified sleep physician interpretation; titration needs desaturation, comorbidity or failed APAP, and APAP is the default; MSLT must follow PSG; HSAT needs symptoms plus absence of comorbidity.
- Florida Blue looser: PSG route (D) needs only one secondary sleep disorder with no symptom requirement; UHC lists RLS unresponsive to treatment as PSG-eligible while Florida Blue excludes RLS entirely; Florida Blue allows physician attestation when baseline studies are missing; Florida Blue's look-back on a non-diagnostic HSAT is 1 year (same as UHC's 12 months), stricter than BCBSM's 3 years.
- Numeric disagreement: COPD (UHC FEV1 <60%; BCBSM FEV1/FVC <=0.7 and FEV1 <80%; Florida Blue "moderate to severe" via PFT, no cutoff); obesity (UHC BMI >=50 contraindicates HSAT; BCBSM BMI >33 with bicarbonate >28; Florida Blue has no BMI contraindication); OHS (Florida Blue pCO2 >45 and pO2 <60; UHC BMI >=30 and PaCO2 >=45; BCBSM PaCO2 >55 sustained or other tests); BMI risk (30 in Florida Blue/BCBSM, 35 in Blue Shield CA); ESS (>=10 Florida Blue, >10 BCBSM/Blue Shield CA). A patient with ESS exactly 10 meets Florida Blue and fails BCBSM and Blue Shield CA.
- Unique to one payer: Blue Shield CA sets required HSAT sensor sets and says HSAT needs no prior auth (policy gates downstream treatment instead); BCBSM requires 8 weeks of EDS before MSLT and annual HGNS surveillance; UHC delegates MSLT to InterQual; Florida Blue embeds HGNS pre-implant criteria (BMI <35, AHI 15-65, <25% central) and a CDL rule; Florida Blue requires an APAP trial before attended titration.

## Structure
- **Layout.** Florida Blue: a two-column table (test name left, criteria right) within a Position Statement; frequency caps and documents are separate in Reimbursement. UHC: a 3-page Coverage Rationale of short bullet lists, then definitions with numeric tables. BCBSM: "Inclusions" blocks per test, with lettered footnotes (a, b, c, d) carrying the contraindication lists. Blue Shield CA: roman-numeral statements (I-IX) whose real thresholds are in prose Policy Guidelines. Cigna 0158: surgical criteria only.
- **Logic shape.** All pathways-style (one rule block per test). Florida Blue writes combination logic in headings: "(A&B, A&C, or D)", "(1 and 2 or 3)". BCBSM has a 2-of-5 inside a 1-of-4. Max nesting is about 4.
- **Where exclusions live.** Florida Blue: the same comorbidity list is a NOT for HSAT and a positive for PSG, split night, titration (copied 6 times with small drifts). BCBSM: as lettered footnotes. UHC: as positive PSG indications. Blue Shield CA: "include, but are not limited to".
- **Delegation.** UHC to InterQual (MSLT) and a separate HGNS policy; Florida Blue to CMS NCD/LCD for Medicare Advantage and NIA site-of-service review; Cigna to a separate diagnostic document; Blue Shield CA to a non-respiratory sleep policy.
- **Codes.** CPT/HCPCS tables in every doc, no ICD-10 anywhere; only Florida Blue and Blue Shield CA tie codes to specific rules in text.

## Problems for an automated checker
- Open-ended lists: Florida Blue "Absence of other comorbid medical conditions or concomitant sleep disorders such as:"; Blue Shield CA "include, but are not limited to significant, ongoing symptoms related to"; UHC "examples include but are not limited to". A tree needs a closed list.
- Derived negation: Florida Blue "Unattended titration using APAP (E0601) meets the definition of medical necessity only when condition A is met and conditions B, C, and D are not."
- Inverted/garbled text: BCBSM "EXCLUSIONS FOR SLEEP STUDIES: When the above criteria are met"; Florida Blue "greater than or greater than or equal to 5 events per hour"; Florida Blue heading "Multiple Latency Test (MSLT) Attended Titration"; unclosed parenthesis in APAP failure criterion.
- Conflicting thresholds between statements in one policy: BCBSM "ESS >10" vs Florida Blue ">= 10"; BCBSM stroke >30 days (HSAT ok) vs stroke within 90 days (CSA suspicion).
- Chart cannot show: "high pretest probability" (Blue Shield CA says it is judgment), "cognitive and physical ability" to do HSAT, "Chronic opioid use when discontinuation is not an option", "valid attempt to retest", "OSA has been evaluated and effectively treated as documented by... PAP download", PAP download data, ABG values, PFT percent predicted, NYHA class; "suspected" diagnoses as contraindications.
- Hidden gates: frequency caps (Florida Blue "two (2) in 12 months") are in Reimbursement, not criteria; Blue Shield CA's HSAT-first rule is in guidance not statements; BCBSM's contraindication list is a footnote.
- Undefined: Blue Shield CA "no established threshold for significant change in weight" but statement depends on it; Florida Blue "Uncontrolled or refractory cardiac arrhythmia"; "Fatigue" alone satisfies a symptom category.
- Parsing: bullets lost, footnote letters glued to words ("HSATa"), page footers inside lists, left-column labels separated from criteria.

## Test cases
1. **54-year-old, ESS exactly 10, habitual loud snoring, BMI 27, no comorbidities, requests HSAT.** Florida Blue approves: category A (ESS >= 10) plus category B (habitual loud snoring), age >= 18, no exclusions. BCBSM denies: ESS must be ">10" and snoring alone is only 1 of the required 2 (BMI 27 and neck size do not count). Blue Shield CA denies: ESS ">10" fails, and snoring alone is one risk factor, not the "any two" for moderate-high pretest probability. UHC approves (any adult with "suspected OSA").
2. **45-year-old, BMI 52, witnessed apneas, ESS 14, serum bicarbonate 25, no lung, heart or neuromuscular disease. Requests in-lab PSG.** UHC approves PSG: "Body mass index (BMI) >= 50" is a listed comorbidity that "prohibits the use of a HSAT". Florida Blue denies PSG and expects HSAT: it has no BMI contraindication, and PSG needs a listed comorbidity (obesity hypoventilation needs pCO2 > 45 and pO2 < 60 on ABG, not present). BCBSM denies PSG: its BMI contraindication needs BMI > 33 AND bicarbonate > 28, and bicarbonate is 25.
3. **Adult with OSA (AHI 20) on APAP, use >= 4 h on 80% of nights for 30 days, residual AHI 3, no desaturation study findings, no comorbidity, requests in-lab titration (95811).** Florida Blue denies: attended titration needs condition A plus (desaturation, or comorbidity or secondary sleep disorder, or failed APAP: residual AHI >= 5 or persisting EDS despite adherence), and APAP is "only when condition A is met and B, C, and D are not." BCBSM denies for the same reason (no APAP contraindication and no failure documented in the CPAP download). UHC would only approve if the individual also meets an attended-PSG criterion (none here). Cigna: not decidable, since the criteria are not in the document supplied.
