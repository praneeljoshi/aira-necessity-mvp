# Vertebroplasty and kyphoplasty (CPT 22510-22515)

Documents: Florida Blue 02-20000-18 (rev 02/15/26; also covers sacroplasty), BCBS Rhode Island (BlueCHiP for Medicare, 04/2019), BCBS Texas RAD601.041 (eff 01/01/2025, end date 12/31/2025), Blue Shield of California 6.01.38 (kyphoplasty only, eff 06/01/2023; vertebroplasty is a separate policy not in the library), UnitedHealthcare commercial 2026T0581Q (eff 03/01/2026) and UHC Community Plan Nebraska CS330NE.F (eff 05/01/2026).

## What it is

Percutaneous injection of bone cement (PMMA) into a fractured or weakened vertebra. Vertebroplasty injects cement directly; kyphoplasty (vertebral augmentation) first creates a cavity with a balloon or mechanical device. Payers cover them for painful osteoporotic compression fractures that did not respond to conservative care, and for painful lesions from myeloma or metastases. They gate on fracture age/duration of failed conservative therapy, imaging confirming an acute fracture and ruling out other pain sources, absence of spinal-cord risk (retropulsion, severe collapse), and often exclude sacroplasty and radiofrequency kyphoplasty as investigational.

## Agreement and disagreement

Agree: the "at least 6 weeks of conservative care" test appears in FL, TX and Blue Shield CA (FL and TX share nearly identical wording, including the 6-week-or-hospitalized vertebroplasty rule); myeloma/metastasis with severe pain is covered by all; RFK and sacroplasty are investigational in every payer that addresses them; collapse below one-third is an exclusion in TX, UHC and a relative contraindication in RI.

Differ: UHC uses an upper bound (within 4 months of onset) and no minimum weeks; TX demands localized tenderness and advanced imaging; RI is the only one with a full contraindication list and the pre-surgical indication; Blue Shield CA is limited to kyphoplasty.

Florida Blue stricter than: UHC on minimum wait (UHC has no minimum weeks), and RI on kyphoplasty (RI states no duration for kyphoplasty). Florida Blue looser than: TX (no imaging, no exam finding, no retropulsion/collapse exclusions, no requirement that oncology patients have failed chemo/radiation), RI (no contraindication list, no debilitating pain wording), UHC (no CT/MRI rule-out of other causes, no collapse/healed-fracture/cord-compression exclusions, no 4-month ceiling, so an old fracture can qualify at FL but not UHC). Florida Blue is looser than TX also for acute fractures: FL covers vertebroplasty for <6 week fractures with hospitalization/ambulation barrier and traumatic fractures after 2 weeks; TX covers the first but calls acute trauma investigational. Florida Blue has no limit on cervical/thoracic level for vertebroplasty; UHC includes cervical.

Unique to Florida Blue: ambulation-preventing criterion, 2-week trauma route, ICD-10 support list. Unique to TX: tenderness to palpation, quantified retropulsion stenosis (>20%) and plana (>90%). Unique to RI: hemangioma/eosinophilic granuloma/pre-surgical use for Medicare, anticoagulation as absolute contraindication. Unique to UHC: 4-month ceiling, steroid-induced fracture, defined terms.

## Structure

FL: one-page Position Statement, bulleted OR-lists per procedure, ICD-10 list in coding, evidence in Description. RI: criteria list plus separate Policy Statement with contraindication lists, definitions in Background. TX: Coverage with an ALL-block nested in the osteoporotic kyphoplasty bullet and a numbered "absence of" list; 3,000 lines of evidence follow. Blue Shield CA: seven numbered policy statements doubling per device. UHC: single Coverage Rationale in three joined blocks (indication AND imaging rule-out AND none of six exclusions), plus Definitions section needed to read it. The UHC Nebraska version is the same criteria with a Community Plan frame and a small documentation paragraph added. Delegation: all point to Medicare LCDs (L34976, L33569) or plan documents; UHC to its Minimally Invasive Spine Surgery policy for sacral work.

## Problems for an automated checker

- Homoglyphs: FL's Description contains evidence quotes with look-alike non-Latin letters (e.g., "vеrtеbrοplаstу"); string search for "vertebroplasty" in that block misses. Criteria are unaffected but quote-matching against the text can fail.
- Vague: "symptomatic", "severe pain", "persistent debilitating pain", "Optimal Medical Therapy" ("Treatments that are used as first line before moving to more invasive, risky, or complex procedures"), "VCFs responding appropriately to conservative therapy", "aggressive features", "major part of the cortical bone".
- And/or: UHC "The following are not present: ... or ... or" (must be none); RI list ends in "and/or" and "and"; TX bullets joined by "and" while sub-list 1-5 is an OR of exclusions.
- Time semantics differ: FL/TX/BSC "at least 6 weeks" (minimum), UHC "within 4 months" (maximum), RI "6 weeks to three months" ("generally"). A single "duration" leaf cannot encode both directions.
- Chart gaps: fracture age is rarely stated; "recent" fracture needs MRI edema or bone scan (RI Background); retropulsion percentage and vertebral height ratio need radiology numbers; "tenderness to palpation" must be in the physical exam.
- TX contradiction: osteoporotic kyphoplasty needs "Advanced imaging studies confirming a non-traumatic, acute compression fracture" yet the document calls "acute vertebral fractures due to osteoporosis" investigational.
- Document currency: TX policy end date 12/31/2025; RI is from 2019; Blue Shield CA lacks its vertebroplasty companion.
- Criteria relying on an evidence section: FL's Description discusses vertebral augmentation after opioid failure but states it only as evidence, not criteria; do not extract from there.
- Open-ended list: UHC "including but not limited to".

## Test cases

1. 78-year-old, T12 osteoporotic compression fracture, 5 weeks of pain, on opioids, cannot walk without assistance, admitted to hospital; MRI edema. Florida Blue: approve vertebroplasty (fracture under 6 weeks that led to hospitalization/prevents ambulation). BCBS TX: approve vertebroplasty (same wording); kyphoplasty would be denied (needs 6 weeks). Blue Shield CA: deny kyphoplasty (needs 6 weeks; acute osteoporotic investigational). UHC: likely approve if pain is within 4 months, Optimal Medical Therapy has failed and CT/MRI ruled out other generators.
2. 72-year-old, L1 osteoporotic fracture 7 months old, pain persists after 12 weeks of analgesics and PT, no imaging rule-out document. Florida Blue: approve kyphoplasty (6 weeks failed, thoracolumbar). UHC: deny (beyond 4 months of pain onset; also no CT/MRI rule-out). BCBS TX: deny (no advanced imaging confirming acute fracture, no tenderness documented, "recent onset" back pain).
3. 66-year-old, L2 osteoporotic fracture, 8 weeks failed conservative care, MRI shows retropulsed fragment narrowing canal 25% and no cord compression. Florida Blue: approve (no imaging exclusions). BCBS TX: deny kyphoplasty (stenosis >20% from retropulsed fragments, retropulsion burst pattern). UHC: deny if "clinical evidence of spinal cord compression" confirmed; otherwise could approve. BCBS RI Medicare: absolute contraindication only if retropulsion compromises the canal with myelopathy; otherwise relative contraindication, judgment call.
