# Pneumatic compression devices (lymphedema pumps)

Documents profiled: BCBSFL 09-E0000-31 (anchor), Cigna 0354 (Compression Devices), BCBSM "Compression Pumps and Appliances for Lymphedema", MedStar Family Choice DC 1419.DC (Medicaid). UHC, Blue Shield CA are not in this library for this treatment.

## What it is
A home pneumatic compression pump inflates a sleeve or garment on an arm, leg or trunk in sequence to push lymph fluid out of the limb. Payers gate it on a diagnosis (chronic lymphedema; Cigna also covers venous stasis ulcers), a trial and failure of conservative care (compression garments, elevation, exercise, sometimes manual drainage), and a device ladder: basic non-programmable pumps first (E0650/E0651), advanced programmable pumps (E0652) only if the basic one is unsuitable or failed, and trunk/chest appliances only after limbs alone failed. Head and neck, arterial disease, DVT prevention and non-pneumatic devices are usually excluded or investigational.

## Agreement and disagreement
**Align:** a 4-week conservative trial is the common number (Cigna, BCBSM, MedStar), a nonprogrammable pump comes before a programmable one, programmable pumps need "unique characteristics" such as scarring or contracture (Florida Blue, BCBSM) or failure of the basic pump (Cigna, MedStar), and head and neck pumps are not covered (Florida Blue, BCBSM, MedStar).

**Florida Blue stricter than others:**
- Florida Blue is the only one to cover venous ulcers as investigational, whereas Cigna covers them after 24 weeks of wound care. A venous ulcer patient is denied at Florida Blue and may be approved at Cigna.
- Florida Blue lists non-pneumatic devices as investigational (Cigna too); BCBSM covers them. A Dayspring request is denied at Florida Blue and Cigna and approved at BCBSM after a failed conservative trial.
- Florida Blue and Cigna both require continued-use evidence; BCBSM and MedStar state none.

**Florida Blue looser:**
- No duration of conservative therapy at all (Cigna, BCBSM, MedStar require 4 weeks), no required garment pressure (BCBSM and MedStar: >=30 mmHg distal), no required components list, no specialist, no severity stage, no 6-month persistence, no measurement requirement.
- Florida Blue puts no extremity-involvement requirement on the trunk/chest pump, whereas MedStar bars trunk-only use and requires extremity involvement for E0652.
- Florida Blue's programmable-limb pump route is a simple "unique characteristics" test, while MedStar additionally requires a 4-week failed non-programmable trial and trunk extension.

**Unique to one payer:** Florida Blue: garment replacement within 6 months and gradient garments for a long list of conditions (varicose veins, venous edema, pregnancy edema, etc.), WHCRA override, post-surgical DVT exception. Cigna: CVI with venous stasis ulcer pathway, 24-week wound care, ICD-10 code gating ("All other codes" not covered), cooling-device exclusion. BCBSM: trunk criteria with 7-item conservative list including diet, medications and anemia correction; non-pneumatic coverage. MedStar: severity findings, ISL stage, lymphedema specialist treating, same-landmark measurement, signature-timing rule, loop that returns the patient to conservative therapy if improved.

## Structure
- **Layout.** Florida Blue: a Description of evidence that is longer than the criteria, then a ~2-page Position Statement of paragraph-per-device-tier; Cigna: 1.5-page Coverage Policy with bullets, then codes and background; BCBSM: Medical Policy Statement then Inclusions/Exclusions with star-footnotes (* and **) defining conservative therapy; MedStar: an administrative numbered outline (B.1.a-h, i-vi).
- **Logic shape.** All pathway style. Florida Blue and BCBSM build a ladder where each device tier points back to the previous ("otherwise eligible for nonprogrammable pumps"), so the tree must reference earlier nodes. MedStar is a deep all-of list (depth 4) plus a looped trial. Cigna has an either/or of two indications with inner all-of.
- **Delegation.** Florida Blue to CMS NCD 280.6/LCD for Medicare Advantage and WHCRA statutory override; MedStar lists LCD/NCD as regulatory references and a separate policy for venous ulcers; Cigna to plan benefit and mandates.
- **Codes.** Florida Blue, Cigna, BCBSM list HCPCS; Cigna and Florida Blue list ICD-10 (Cigna uses them as a gate); MedStar names only E0650-E0652 inline.

## Problems for an automated checker
- Undefined "failure": Florida Blue "lymphedema that has failed to respond to conservative measures, such as elevation of the limb and use of compression garments"; BCBSM "demonstrating failed responses to treatment"; Cigna "intractable lymphedema". No volume or percentage threshold exists in any document; MedStar only says "no improvement".
- And/or ambiguity: Florida Blue "otherwise eligible ... AND • unique characteristics ... OR • inadequate response" (A AND (B OR C) or (A AND B) OR C); Cigna "ALL of" containing "compression bandage or compression garment".
- Self-waiving components: BCBSM "Elevation where appropriate", "Manual lymphatic drainage (where available)", "Medications as appropriate".
- Contradictions inside a document: MedStar items iv and v are duplicates; its E0652 requires both unique characteristics and truncal extension (c and d) as written; BCBSM trunk criteria lists "failed conservative therapy" and "failed limb pumps" where the first already includes the pump trial.
- Chart cannot show: garment pressure ">= 30 mmHg distally" (charts rarely record), "Self-MLD for at least 30 minutes per day", "ability of member/caregiver to apply the device", "signature date on or before the prescription date", whether the provider is "a lymphedema specialist", measurement "with reference to the same anatomic landmarks", "adherence" (no threshold), "objective evidence of improvement".
- Extraction: Florida Blue's long garment code list; Cigna's ICD-10 ranges and "All other codes"; BCBSM's star-footnotes; MedStar footnote glyph; evidence-review prose that sounds like criteria (Florida Blue Description, Cigna background about E0652 for trunk).
- Mismatch: BCBSM library metadata says effective 11/1/2022 but the document prints 11/1/25; BCBSM lists E1399 as both established (non-pneumatic table) and investigational.

## Test cases
1. **58-year-old, chronic leg lymphedema after cancer surgery, 2 weeks of compression stockings and elevation, no improvement, wants E0651.** Florida Blue approves (no duration, "failed to respond to conservative measures, such as elevation and compression garments"). Cigna denies (needs failure of a four-week trial). BCBSM denies (four-week trial with exercise and >= 30 mmHg compression). MedStar denies (four-week trial with all six components, specialist, severity findings).
2. **70-year-old with chronic venous insufficiency, venous stasis ulcer of the calf, 26 weeks of supervised compression and dressings, ulcer unchanged, no lymphedema diagnosis.** Cigna approves E0650/E0651 (CVI with ulcer: 24 weeks of supervised standard wound care and no improvement). Florida Blue denies (venous ulcer treatment is investigational; indication limited to lymphedema). BCBSM denies (policy is for lymphedema; no venous ulcer criteria). MedStar routes to policy 1420 and denies under 1419.
3. **Patient with arm lymphedema extending onto the chest wall, failed limb-only pump trial of 6 weeks, wants a programmable pump with trunk appliance; no scarring.** Florida Blue: trunk nonprogrammable pump approved after failure of conservative measures and limb-only pump; programmable trunk pump needs "unique characteristics" OR "inadequate response to an initial course" with a nonprogrammable trunk pump, so denied at the programmable step (approve nonprogrammable trunk first). BCBSM approves trunk/chest pump (lymphedema beyond limbs, failed conservative with 4-week E0650/E0651, failed limb-only). MedStar approves E0652 only if the patient also has unique characteristics (scarring or contracture), so denies. Cigna: no separate trunk criteria; E0652 requires failure of the standard device, which was met, so approves.
