# Sleep apnea surgery

Documents profiled: Florida Blue 02-40000-16 (anchor), Cigna 0158 (surgical treatments for OSA), UnitedHealthcare 2026T0525VV (commercial/exchange), Kaiser Permanente Mid-Atlantic HNS policy, BCBS Michigan Medicare Advantage OSA/snoring surgery policy. The BCBS Michigan file (named "hypoglossal-nerve-stimulation-for-osa") contains NO clinical criteria; it only points to CMS LCDs. Kaiser covers only hypoglossal nerve stimulation (HGNS). Florida Blue is the broadest: oral appliances, conventional surgery, HGNS, and investigational lists in one policy.

## What it is

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is repeated collapse of the upper airway during sleep. Surgery tries to widen or stabilize the airway: uvulopalatopharyngoplasty (UPPP, palate), hyoid suspension and tongue procedures, maxillomandibular advancement (MMA, jaw), and, now the most contested, hypoglossal nerve stimulation (HGNS, an implanted device that moves the tongue forward with each breath). Payers gate it on: a sleep-study diagnosis with an AHI (apnea-hypopnea index) threshold, failed or refused positive airway pressure (PAP/CPAP) therapy, an anatomic finding that locates the obstruction (drug-induced sleep endoscopy, DISE, for HGNS), and for HGNS a BMI ceiling and a limit on central apneas. Snoring alone and most minimally invasive "office" procedures are uniformly not covered.

## Agreement and disagreement

Where they align
- HGNS rules are one family across the four payers that rule on it: FDA-approved device, AHI >= 15, central/mixed apneas about 25% or less of the AHI, no complete concentric collapse at the soft palate on DISE, failed or not-tolerated PAP. Florida Blue, Cigna and UHC agree on AHI 15-100 (Kaiser lowers it to 65).
- The Down syndrome adolescent pathway is nearly identical in Florida Blue and Cigna: 13-18 years, AHI > 10 and < 50, BMI at or below the 95th percentile, adenotonsillectomy contraindicated or failed, PAP failure.
- Palate/tongue radiofrequency, tongue-base suspension, palatal stiffening, and stand-alone uvulectomy or snoring treatment are rejected (investigational or not medically necessary) by Florida Blue, Cigna and UHC where they speak to them. Phrenic nerve stimulation for central sleep apnea: not covered at Florida Blue and UHC.
- Conventional surgery always follows a PAP or appliance trial.

Where Florida Blue is STRICTER
- HGNS BMI ceiling is 35. Cigna (Inspire) and UHC allow 40; Cigna (Genio) is stricter at 32. Kaiser is "less than 35" (so 35 itself fails there).
- PAP failure wording: Florida Blue accepts "CPAP failure ... or inability to tolerate CPAP". Cigna and UHC explicitly accept "unwillingness" or "refusal"; Florida Blue does not name refusal for adult HGNS (it does in the Down syndrome pathway: "refusal to use the device").
- Hyoid, tongue and MMA surgery require "objective documentation of hypopharyngeal obstruction" (method unspecified); this is a documentation burden Cigna and UHC place differently (retropalatal/multisite narrowing; craniofacial deformity).
- Revision/replacement of the whole HGNS system requires the original criteria to be met again plus out-of-warranty. Kaiser needs only malfunction, irreparability or lapsed warranty (any one).

Where Florida Blue is LOOSER
- Severity: Florida Blue's "clinically significant OSA" is AHI >= 15, or AHI >= 5 with one symptom or comorbidity (even hypertension). UHC requires AHI/RDI >= 15 AND Epworth > 10 for UPPP/MMA; Cigna names no AHI for UPPP.
- Prior therapy for UPPP: one of CPAP failure OR oral appliance failure suffices. Cigna requires PAP failure, intolerance or unwillingness AND that an appliance was considered.
- MMA: Florida Blue has no severity or craniofacial requirement; Cigna requires "severe OSA" and "craniofacial disproportion or deformities"; UHC requires craniofacial disproportion with maxillomandibular deficiency.
- Sleep-study type: UHC mandates attended PSG for surgical criteria; Florida Blue accepts AHI, REI or RDI (home tests count).
- AHI ceiling 100 vs Kaiser 65 and Cigna Genio 65. No contraindication list, tonsil grade, shared-decision-making step or sleep study recency (Kaiser: 12 months within 24 months).
- Hyoid suspension: Florida Blue covers it with criteria; UHC says "Isolated hyoid myotomy" is unproven.
- Tracheotomy: Florida Blue's Down syndrome pathway accepts "either a tracheotomy or are ineffectively treated with CPAP"; UHC requires "Absence of tracheostomy use during sleep".

Unique to one payer
- Florida Blue: adenotonsillectomy criteria with pediatric AHI (>= 5, or >= 1.5 with symptoms); intraoral appliance criteria (TMD/periodontal disease absent, dental personnel); one appliance per 12 months; ICD-10 G47.30-G47.39; LOINC time-frame table.
- Cigna: DISE medical necessity (adults, Down syndrome, children); device-specific tables (Inspire vs Genio with different age/AHI/BMI); remote replacement; multi-level/stepwise surgery.
- UHC: Epworth > 10; attended-PSG footnote and repeat-PSG rule; InterQual delegation for oral appliances; caregiver refusal of MMA; "qualified physician or APP" evaluation.
- Kaiser: shared decision-making, 18 contraindications, tonsil grade, machine-derived adherence (>= 70% of nights in a month), sleep study windows.
- BCBSM MA: nothing clinical; CMS-first hierarchy.

## Structure

- Layout. Florida Blue: Description (with evidence), Position Statement, Coding, LOINC table, Program Exceptions, Definitions (carrying thresholds). Cigna: 4 pages of policy then 20 pages of background; criteria sit in "Coverage Policy" bullets and a device table. UHC: criteria on pp.1-3 with definitions on pp.3-4 and 17 pages of evidence. Kaiser: six-page numbered policy (III.A criteria, III.B documentation, IV.B contraindications, V replacement). BCBSM: a delegation memo with a code table.
- Logic shape. Florida Blue and Cigna are pathways per procedure, each an all-of; HGNS adult and Down syndrome are separate all-of blocks. UHC: one all-of for UPPP/osteotomy/MMA plus per-procedure "in addition" conditions, then HGNS blocks. Kaiser: single numbered all-of (11 items) plus a NOT list. Cigna's PAP clause is a three-way any-of repeated six times; Cigna's HGNS block has a device-by-device any-of (each device row an all-of of age/AHI/BMI).
- Delegation. Florida Blue to separate PAP and sleep-testing policies and (Medicare Advantage) NCD 160.19, LCD L38398, LCD L33611. Cigna to the Sleep Disordered Breathing policy for HSAT/PSG and to "delegated vendor guidelines" in some markets. UHC to InterQual (oral appliances) and the Sleep Studies policy. Kaiser to the Medicare Coverage Database for Medicare members; MCG ACG A-0973 cited in references. BCBSM to CMS NCD 160.7 and MAC-specific LCDs (WPS, CGS, First Coast, Noridian, Novitas, Palmetto, Wellpoint), chosen by jurisdiction.
- Codes. Florida Blue: one CPT/HCPCS list with "(Investigational)" tags on some rows plus ICD-10 G47.30-G47.39. Cigna: grouped by status heading. UHC: all codes (surgical, DME) in one table that states listing implies nothing. Kaiser: none. BCBSM: a code table with no criteria.

## Problems for an automated checker

1. Florida Blue definitions overlap: "Mild to Moderate Obstructive Sleep Apnea" is defined as "AHI, REI, or RDI of 15 or more events per hour", which includes every severe case (> 30). A patient with AHI 45 is both "mild to moderate" and "severe". Oral appliances: "For members with mild to moderate OSA; OR For members with severe OSA who have failed a trial of CPAP or is contraindicated" is therefore partly redundant.
2. Florida Blue's "clinically significant OSA" is defined in the Definitions section, far from the criteria that cite it; the adult and pediatric definitions have the same name.
3. "failed an adequate trial of CPAP", "appropriately selected members", "have not responded to" are undefined, while the HGNS block defines failure numerically ("residual AHI >= 15 or failure to use CPAP >= 4 hr per night for >= 5 nights per week"). A checker must use different standards for the same word inside one policy.
4. CPAP adherence numbers differ: Florida Blue ">= 4 hr per night for >= 5 nights per week", Cigna "> 4 hours of use per night, 5 nights per week", Kaiser "less than 4 hours a night for at least 70% of the nights in 1 month". Strict vs non-strict inequality (">= 4" vs "> 4") and failure of "AHI >= 15" vs "AHI > 15" within one Cigna document.
5. UHC: "Total AHI of < 25% for central and mixed Apneas" is garbled; the Down syndrome line mixes AHI (>= 10) and RDI (<= 50) and calls the diagnosis "severe OSA" while severe is defined as > 30.
6. UHC criteria lines lose their bullet glyphs in text extraction; an asterisk footnote ("Polysomnography should be repeated if...") carries a criterion at the bottom; "another validated tool" for sleepiness has no cutoff.
7. Cigna: "criteria for PAP met" refers to another policy; "consideration has also been given to use of mandibular repositioning appliance" is satisfied by a note that it was thought about; the device table (age/AHI/BMI per device) flattens in text and loses row membership.
8. Kaiser: criterion 1 (age 18 or older) and criterion 11 ("Patients under age 18 will be considered on a case-by-case basis") sit in the same all-of; contraindications IV.B repeat positive criteria (BMI >= 35; central > one-quarter). The "Shared Decision-Making ... who determines that the patient demonstrates CPAP failure" criterion is a process, not a chart fact.
9. Kaiser contraindications use open terms: "Severe co-morbid condition", "Severe insomnia", "Acute psychiatric disorder or unstable psychological status".
10. BCBSM: no criteria. A naive extractor will turn the Description sentence "when conservative therapies or CPAP have failed" or the code table into a rule set. The real rule is in an LCD selected by MAC jurisdiction.
11. Criteria a chart often cannot show: DISE findings (a separate procedure report), "ineffectively treated with CPAP due to noncompliance, discomfort, undesirable side effects, persistent symptoms despite compliance use, or refusal" (CPAP download needed), "Contraindicated for or not effectively treated by adenotonsillectomy", "craniofacial disproportion", tonsil grade, central apnea percentage (needs the PSG scoring table).
12. Many "meets criteria" statements sit adjacent to experimental lists with the word "or" ("palate and mandible expansion devices") that look like criteria.

## Test cases

1. BMI 37 HGNS candidate. 52-year-old man, AHI 38 on attended PSG, central + mixed 8% of AHI, BMI 37, nightly CPAP use averaging 5 hours but residual AHI 22 on CPAP, DISE shows no complete concentric collapse. UHC approves (BMI <= 40, AHI 15-100, central < 25%, "PAP therapy resulted in no therapeutic efficacy"). Cigna approves for Inspire (BMI <= 40). Florida Blue denies: "Body mass index <= 35 kg/m2". Kaiser denies: "BMI is less than 35 kg/m2" and BMI >= 35 is a listed contraindication.
2. Symptomatic AHI 11 UPPP candidate. 36-year-old woman, AHI 11 on a home sleep test, Epworth 14, hypertensive, failed an oral-appliance trial, DISE/exam shows retropalatal narrowing. Florida Blue approves UPPP: "AHI, REI, or RDI of 5 or more events per hour in a member with one or more signs or symptoms associated with OSA" and "failed an adequate trial of an oral appliance". UHC denies: "Moderate to severe OSA ... AHI >= 15 or RDI >= 15, as determined by Polysomnography (Attended)" and a home test does not qualify. (Cigna's UPPP block states no AHI; it would still need PAP failure/intolerance/unwillingness, which this patient has not shown.)
3. Very severe OSA HGNS candidate. 58-year-old man, AHI 80 on PSG, BMI 30, central 5%, CPAP downloads show 3 h/night on 40% of nights after mask and pressure optimization, then machine returned; DISE without complete concentric collapse; sleep study 6 months ago. Florida Blue approves ("AHI >= 15 and <= 100", BMI <= 35, "inability to tolerate CPAP"). UHC approves (AHI <= 100, CPAP intolerance). Cigna approves for Inspire (15-100) but not Genio (15-65). Kaiser denies: sleep study must show "AHI of 15-65 events per hour".
