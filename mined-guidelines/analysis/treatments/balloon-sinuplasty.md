# Balloon sinuplasty: four payers compared

Documents: Florida Blue 02-31000-01 (rev. 08/15/26, 10 pp), Pennsylvania Health and Wellness / Centene PA.CP.MP.119 (last review 10/19, 6 pp), UnitedHealthcare Commercial 2019T0571G (eff. 11/1/2019, 12 pp), Cigna 0480 (eff. 4/15/2026, 28 pp, also covers Eustachian tube dilation). Profiles are in `sourcing/analysis/profiles/`.

## What it is

Balloon sinus ostial dilation (balloon sinuplasty, CPT 31295-31298) threads a balloon catheter into the opening (ostium) of the maxillary, frontal or sphenoid sinus and inflates it to widen drainage, instead of cutting tissue as in functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS). It can be done alone or with FESS. The three clinical payers gate it on: a diagnosis of chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS, symptoms for more than 12 weeks) or recurrent acute rhinosinusitis (RARS, four or more episodes a year); symptoms; CT evidence of sinus disease; failure of a defined medical regimen (antibiotics, steroid nasal spray, saline); and not being an excluded situation (ethmoid disease, polyps). Florida Blue is different in kind: its guideline contains no clinical criteria at all.

## Agreement and disagreement

Where they align:
- Centene, UHC and Cigna all use a CRS pathway (12 weeks or 3 months), a RARS pathway (4 or more episodes a year), CT confirmation, and a medical-therapy failure test.
- All three accept maxillary, frontal and sphenoid sinuses only, and none covers other uses.
- All three require steroid spray and an antibiotic (antibiotic conditional at UHC).

Where they differ:
- Medical therapy: Centene sets minimums for antibiotics (3 weeks) and nasal steroid (4 weeks); UHC sets no durations; Cigna requires everything for 8 consecutive weeks and adds an antihistamine/decongestant and saline.
- CT: UHC and Cigna want it in the sinus (each sinus) to be dilated; Centene is not per sinus. The RARS CT test is "and/or" at UHC and "and" at Cigna.
- Endoscopy: required in Cigna RARS only, optional for Centene children.
- Polyps: UHC excludes "nasal polyps or tumors" while Cigna accepts "nasal polyposis" as a CT finding and Centene accepts polyps on CT.
- Ethmoid: Centene excludes ethmoid disease; the others do not mention it.
- Prior surgery: only Centene excludes extensive prior surgery.
- Age: only Centene has age bands.
- Bundling with FESS: Florida Blue (entire policy) and Cigna (one sentence).

Unique to one payer: Centene ICD-10 list, culture-guided antibiotic option, etiology clause; UHC documentation table and the per-code "treatment" list; Cigna Eustachian tube criteria and endoscopy for RARS; Florida Blue LOINC documentation table and device investigational statement.

Florida Blue stricter or looser:
- Looser in clinical terms: no CRS/RARS definition, symptom, CT, medical-therapy, or exclusion test. A chart is never tested against clinical criteria.
- Stricter on payment: concurrent dilation with FESS is "not separately reimbursable", as at Cigna; UHC and Centene permit both.
- Stricter on implants: all implantable sinus devices are investigational. The other three do not address them (Cigna refers to another policy).
- Stand-alone dilation is neither approved nor denied; the policy is silent, so Florida Blue is neither stricter nor looser in a testable sense.

## Structure

- Layout: Florida Blue is the standard Florida Blue template (description, position statement, coding with a LOINC table, program exceptions, references). Centene is a short clinical policy with an outline (I.A.1.a.i). UHC is a one-page coverage rationale plus a documentation table, then 8 pages of evidence. Cigna is a 2-page policy statement covering two procedures, then about 17 pages of background.
- Logic shape: Centene is ALL (A, B, C) with A an any-of of CRS or RARS and age-banded sub-branches; UHC is any-of two ALL lists; Cigna is the same shape with a k-of-n symptom test.
- Delegation: Florida Blue to FEP/SAO guidelines and a drug policy for Sinuva; UHC to the member plan document and a related FESS policy (and notes MCG may be used); Cigna to related policies and "delegated vendor guidelines" in some markets; Centene none.
- Codes: all four list 31295-31298; Centene alone ties ICD-10 codes to the criteria; Cigna ties CPT to "criteria met"; UHC and Florida Blue only list.

## Problems for an automated checker

- Florida Blue has nothing to extract: "...is considered integral to the traditional nasal/sinus endoscopic surgery or functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) when performed at the same time and is not separately reimbursable." A chart cannot demonstrate "reimbursable"; the checker must decide whether concurrency with FESS can be read from the order, and the Description sentence "fail medical management (e.g., mucolytic, decongestant (oral and topical), antibiotic therapy)" is not a criterion even though it looks like one.
- The LOINC time-frame rule ("six months or fewer before starting date of service") is a documentation window, not a clinical one.
- Centene age split: "If > 18 years of age ... If ≤ 18 years of age" puts an 18-year-old in the pediatric branch. Item A.1.a.ii requires a CT showing "Polyps in nasal cavity or the middle meatus, and/or sinus opacification", with "and/or" ambiguity. The antibiotic example "Beta-lactamase resistant antibiotic ... (e.g., amoxicillin [recommended]...)" lists plain amoxicillin as an example.
- Centene "Allergic or immune etiologies ... ruled out or treated appropriately" is rarely in a chart; silence will be INSUFFICIENT.
- UHC: "full courses of all of the following treatments: Antibiotic therapy, if bacterial infection is suspected, Nasal lavage, and Intranasal corticosteroids" has a conditional item and no durations; "distinct symptom free intervals" and "Recent physical exam" are undefined. The documentation table lists CPT codes in a merged cell that breaks across a page. "Nasal polyps or tumors" conflicts with CT findings that count in the CRS pathway.
- Cigna: "failure, intolerance or contraindication of medical management when ALL the following have been tried" needs a rule for a contraindication to one of the four items; Background says "at least two antibiotics" while criteria say only "antibiotic therapy"; "each of the sinuses" means the criteria must be evaluated per sinus requested, and the order may name a CPT code covering two sinuses (31298).
- Criteria a chart cannot show: whether bacterial infection was "suspected"; "ruled out" allergy; "eight consecutive weeks" of a regimen unless dated notes show start and end of each drug.
- Cigna's ETBD criteria are in the same document; a checker keyed on this PDF must not apply them to a sinus request.

## Test cases

1. 45-year-old, 8 months of nasal obstruction and facial pressure; CT shows mucosal thickening in the maxillary sinus; 4 weeks of amoxicillin-clavulanate, 6 weeks of fluticasone, saline rinses used for 3 weeks, no antihistamine spray. Centene: approves (symptom + CT + antibiotic 3 weeks + steroid 4 weeks, assuming allergy addressed). UHC: approves if the notes call the courses "full" (antibiotic, lavage, steroid all present; CT thickening in the sinus to be dilated). Cigna: denies (criteria require antihistamine spray and/or decongestant too, and all four tried for at least 8 consecutive weeks; saline was only 3 weeks).
2. 30-year-old with 5 episodes of acute sinusitis in the past year with symptom-free intervals; CT shows mucosal thickening but the ostium is patent; no nasal endoscopy. UHC: approves RARS (CT "ostial occlusion and/or mucosal thickening", "and/or" satisfied by thickening). Cigna: denies (RARS requires nasal endoscopy findings and CT evidence of ostial occlusion AND mucosal thickening). Centene: needs documented bacterial episodes and purulent discharge plus a medical regimen.
3. 52-year-old CRS patient scheduled for FESS with balloon dilation of the maxillary ostia in the same operation, with full criteria met. UHC and Centene: approve (dilation permitted alone or with FESS). Cigna: dilation in the same sinus cavity as FESS is "integral" and not separately reimbursable, so the dilation is not paid separately. Florida Blue: same bundling ("integral ... when performed at the same time"), no separate payment, and no clinical criteria to satisfy at all.
