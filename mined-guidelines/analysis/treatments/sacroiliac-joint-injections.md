# Sacroiliac joint injections: cross-payer analysis

Documents profiled (profiles in `sourcing/analysis/profiles/`): Florida Blue 02-20000-21 (11 pp.), BCBS Montana SUR705.043 (16 pp.), Blue Shield of California BSC_EVO_1756 (12 pp.), Evolent Clinical Guideline 1756 (pp.57-66 of a 102-page compendium), Carelon Interventional Pain Management (SI section pp.24-27 of 34), UnitedHealthcare 2026T0621O (12 pp.).

## What it is

An SI joint injection puts local anesthetic, usually with a corticosteroid, into (or around) the sacroiliac joint under imaging. It is used two ways: diagnostic (does relief after an anesthetic-only block prove the joint is the pain source?) and therapeutic (steroid for longer relief). Payers gate it on how long the pain has lasted, a failed course of conservative care, proof the pain is really from the SI joint (physical-exam provocation tests, or a positive diagnostic block), imaging guidance, and limits on how many injections are allowed and how well the previous one worked. Several payers also fence it off from lateral branch blocks and radiofrequency ablation, which they treat as different procedures.

## Agreement and disagreement

Where they agree: pain of about 3 months; conservative care before injection (6 weeks is the common number); imaging guidance required; at least 50% relief to justify a repeat; two diagnostic injections at most; lateral branch blocks and RF are not part of this service; a numeric annual cap of 3 or 4.

Where they differ:
- Proof of SI origin. Blue Shield CA/Evolent want 3 of 8 provocation tests; Carelon wants tenderness plus 1 test; Florida Blue and Montana ask for none in the criteria.
- Pain severity. Only BSC/Evolent (>=6/10 or disability) and Carelon (>=3/10 with 2 ADLs) set a score. Florida Blue sets none.
- Role of diagnostic blocks. Florida Blue allows two diagnostic injections as a normal step. Carelon, BSC and Evolent allow them only for surgical planning; Carelon forbids steroid in them. Montana demands dual blocks with two agents.
- Relief durability for repeat: Florida Blue and Carelon 6 weeks; BSC/Evolent 2 months; Montana none.
- Cap: Florida Blue 3 per joint per 6 months (soft); Montana 3 per year; Carelon 3 per 12 months plus 3-month spacing; BSC/Evolent 4 per 12 months per region.
- Ultrasound: Florida Blue calls it E/I; BSC/Evolent allow it as alternative; Carelon only in pregnancy.

Unique to one payer: Florida Blue's ICD-10 support list, 2-week "worsening pain" fast track, and the "comprehensive pain treatment plan" item; Montana's behavioral/addiction evaluation and dual-agent blocks; BSC/Evolent's spondyloarthropathy pathway, contraindication lists, authorization per injection, 2-year reset and no-telehealth exam; Carelon's volume limits (1.5 cc / 2.0 cc), independent-radiologist rule and radiculopathy exclusion; UHC's site-of-service review; Evolent's Washington mandate.

Florida Blue vs the others:
- Looser: no exam findings, no pain score, no radiculopathy exclusion, no contraindication list; 2-week alternative path; diagnostic injections not limited to surgical candidates; steroid not barred in diagnostic phase; 1-week spacing for diagnostic injections; repeat relief only 6 weeks (BSC/Evolent want 2 months).
- Stricter: requires all three conservative elements (NSAID, activity modification, PT/chiro/HEP) with specific durations (Carelon needs PT plus any one complementary item; Montana's list is similar but with PT 6 wk); NSAIDs for >=4 weeks; "comprehensive pain treatment plan"; ultrasound is E/I; cap measured over a short 6-month window (3 per joint) is tighter in the short run than BSC's 4 per 12 months, though it is a trigger for medical review rather than a denial; injections that "are not effective" are not necessary.

## Structure

- Florida Blue: classic MCG, criteria in the Position Statement, the cap hides in Reimbursement Information, and a LOINC table lists acceptable evidence. Bulleted ALL with nested sub-bullets, an OR at the same level as the AND-list. ICD-10 listed as "support", not enforced.
- Montana: Coverage section as two ALL lists; definitions (NOTE 1/2) duplicated in Policy Guidelines; large evidence review. Logic is short but defined through notes.
- Blue Shield CA and Evolent: vendor guideline (formerly NIA) adopted nearly verbatim; pathways with nested k-of-n; phases (initial / therapeutic) carry state. Evolent adds a state mandate and sits inside a 102-page compendium; BSC sits in a 12-page policy with an extra rationale "Special Note" holding a criterion.
- Carelon: general definitions section applies to all pain procedures in the guideline; specific rules override general ones; redlined PDF.
- UHC: pure delegation to InterQual; policy text covers fusion mostly.

## Problems for an automated checker

- UHC: "Sacroiliac Joint (SI) injections are proven and medically necessary in certain circumstances." There is no criteria text to extract.
- Florida Blue OR/AND scope: "Continued pain after 6 weeks with ALL of the following ... OR Worsening pain after 2 weeks with ALL ...". The OR is at the same bullet level as the top-level ANDs, and the 3-month pain requirement conflicts with a 2-week trial.
- Florida Blue "if not contraindicated", "comprehensive pain treatment plan", "Activity modification": no definitions.
- Florida Blue cap is outside the position statement and says "subject to medical review".
- Montana: "which may consist of therapies such as" versus NOTE 1 "should include" (is it optional or required?). NOTE 2 defines SI blocks using "lateral branch blocks". "There is no consensus on whether a minimum of 50% or 75% reduction" leaves the diagnostic threshold open.
- BSC/Evolent: "50% pain relief or significant documented functional improvement" mixes a number with an undefined judgment; superscript reference numbers like (1,2,3,4) look like data; the 2-year reset sits in Rationale/Special Note.
- Carelon: redline residue ("20243", "04/14/2024 04/09/2023", "rootneural") and conflicting definitions of conservative management; "The specific requirements take precedence over any stated general requirement."
- Chart-invisible facts: average pain score over time, percent relief and exact duration of relief, which anesthetic and its expected duration, number of provocation tests, counts of prior injections per joint/region, NSAID duration, HEP follow-up, whether the injection was "part of a comprehensive plan".
- State mandates (Washington) change criteria by member location.

## Test cases

1. Four months of SI pain at 4/10. Six weeks of NSAIDs, activity change and a prescribed home program with follow-up; one positive FABER test; no radiculopathy. Florida Blue approves: pain over 3 months, all three conservative elements for 6 weeks, no exam or score requirement. Blue Shield CA/Evolent deny: only 1 of 3 required provocation tests and pain below 6 (unless functional disability is documented). Carelon would approve on pain 4/10 if two ADLs are impaired.
2. Prior SI steroid injection 10 weeks ago gave 60% relief for 7 weeks; patient requests a second. Florida Blue approves: relief >=50% for >=6 weeks and 2 injections within 6 months. Carelon denies: repeats may not be less than 3 months apart. Blue Shield CA/Evolent deny: relief must last at least 2 months.
3. Pregnant patient (or one for whom radiation is a documented concern) with 4 months of SI pain who has met every other criterion, requesting an ultrasound-guided steroid injection. Florida Blue denies: "Sacroiliac joint injection performed with ultrasound guidance is considered experimental or investigational." Carelon approves for pregnancy ("Ultrasound is the only imaging guidance appropriate for use during pregnancy"); Blue Shield CA/Evolent approve where fluoroscopy or CT is contraindicated or radiation is problematic.
