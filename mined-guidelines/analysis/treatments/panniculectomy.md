# Panniculectomy: five payers compared

Documents: Florida Blue 02-12000-16 (rev 03/15/26, 8 pp); BCBS Michigan joint policy (eff 3/1/26, 9 pp); Cigna 0027 (eff 6/15/2026, 10 pp); Excellus BCBS 7.01.53 (eff May 21, 2026, 11 pp, includes lipedema); Kaiser Permanente Mid-Atlantic "Redundant Skin Surgery including Panniculectomy" (approval history to 03/26/2025, 6 pp).

## What it is

Panniculectomy removes the hanging apron of abdominal skin and fat (the pannus), without muscle tightening or moving the umbilicus. Payers treat it as reconstructive only when the pannus causes a documented medical problem, and cosmetic otherwise. They gate it on pannus size (the ASPS grade 1-5 scale or a landmark), a skin condition under the fold that fails months of medical therapy, functional limits, and, after big weight loss or bariatric surgery, proof that weight has stabilised. Abdominoplasty (tummy tuck) is cosmetic for every payer here.

## Agreement and disagreement

Agreement: all (except BCBSM's commercial text) use a 3-month failed-medical-therapy rule; all require the 18-month wait after bariatric surgery (Florida Blue's alternative aside); abdominoplasty and diastasis repair are cosmetic everywhere; back/neck pain does not qualify.

Where Florida Blue is looser:
- Three alternative symptom routes: a skin condition (3 months), chronic maceration "refractory" (no length), or just "documented difficulty with ambulation". Others require the skin problem with 3 months of failed treatment (Cigna, Excellus, Kaiser) or "uncontrolled intertrigo" (BCBSM). The functional impairment route alone needs no skin disease at all.
- Post-bariatric patients can qualify at "at least 18 months post-operative OR documented stable weight for at least 3 months"; Cigna, Excellus and Kaiser require both 18 months and 6 months stable weight.
- Stable weight is 3 months (others 6) and attempts at weight loss that were "unsuccessful" can substitute for stable loss.
- Pannus size is the same grade >= 2 as Excellus and Kaiser; Cigna ("at or below the symphysis pubis") and BCBSM ("below the level of the pubis") use landmarks, not grades, so a chart graded 2 may or may not satisfy them.
- Photographs "may be required", versus Excellus "absolute requirement" and Cigna's photographs.

Where Florida Blue is stricter:
- Functional deficit AND ADL interference AND expected improvement are all required in addition to one of the three options (Cigna requires them too; BCBSM and Kaiser require fewer).
- A pannus resulting from a contract-excluded bariatric procedure is excluded outright.
- Covers concurrent surgery only in "extremely rare circumstances"; Kaiser covers it more broadly.
- Excellus demands systemic therapy; Florida Blue says "oral and topical" in the first option only.

Unique: Kaiser's nutrition-class requirement at BMI > 35 and renal-transplant indication; Excellus's BMI <= 30 / %EWL / 100 lb alternatives and lipedema criteria; BCBSM's >= 100 lb loss path with only 6 months; Florida Blue's "significant weight loss" definition in a note and the contract-exclusion rule.

## Structure

Florida Blue is an MCG with a base ALL list, a stacked weight-loss ALL list that re-includes the base, a cosmetic section, and a LOINC documentation table. BCBSM is a two-path "ONE of" over two "ALL" lists, with the Medicare LCD (which has different timing) reproduced in the Government Regulations section. Cigna has a compact ALL list where each item is tagged with the document that must prove it (photographs vs H&P). Excellus uses roman numeral/letter nesting to five levels and moves documentation rules into "Policy Guidelines". Kaiser spreads criteria across III (criteria), IV (restrictions that add nutrition and weight rules) and V (exclusions), and covers several body regions. All delegate benefit questions to the contract; Kaiser references MCG ACG A-0498 only in the bibliography; Florida Blue and BCBSM reference Medicare LCDs.

## Problems for an automated checker

- Florida Blue: "ALL ... AND One of the following ... AND When ALL of the following" is three blocks with an OR in the middle; the third option "functional impairment, such as documented difficulty with ambulation" overlaps with the later "functional deficit" ALL block.
- "Significant weight loss varies based on the member's clinical circumstances and may be documented when..." (Florida Blue): "may" makes the three definitions examples; the trigger for the weight-loss branch is not mechanical.
- "stable weight" for 3 vs 6 months, measured relative to what date (Excellus: "prior to the consultation"); a chart needs dated weights.
- Pannus grade must be read from photographs; a chart note saying "large pannus" does not give a grade; BCBSM and Cigna use landmarks (pubis, symphysis).
- "failed both oral and topical medications" and "recurrent chronic and persistent skin condition" are clinical judgments; Excellus requires documented adherence, which charts rarely hold.
- "severe physical deformity or disfigurement" (Florida Blue, Cigna): undefined.
- BCBSM: "uncontrolled intertrigo (which is unresponsive to conservative therapy...)" has no duration in the commercial text; LCD text in the same file says 3 months and 6 months stable. An extractor reading the whole document would merge two criteria sets.
- Kaiser's III.B parenthetical is garbled ("hernia repair that are reducible and have associated symptoms"), and its BMI > 35 rule appears in a separate "Restrictions" section.
- Florida Blue's rule that a pannus from a contract-excluded bariatric procedure is excluded depends on benefit data, not the chart.
- Reference sections contain numeric evidence (complication rates) that is not criteria.

## Test cases

1. 45-year-old, grade 2 pannus on photographs, 2 months of topical antifungals for intertrigo, difficulty walking one block, no weight loss. Florida Blue could approve (third option, documented difficulty with ambulation, plus ADL block). Cigna, Excellus and Kaiser deny (3 months of failed therapy required). BCBSM denies (no uncontrolled intertrigo).
2. 38-year-old, bariatric surgery 20 months ago, weight stable for 4 months, grade 3 pannus, 4 months of failed topical and oral therapy with ADL interference. Florida Blue approves (post-op >= 18 months and weight stable >= 3 months, with the skin and ADL criteria met). Cigna and Excellus deny (stable 6 months required); Kaiser denies (6 months stable).
3. 52-year-old, BMI 38, grade 2 pannus, cellulitis recurring, 5 months of failed therapy, ADL interference, no weight loss. Florida Blue and Cigna could approve. Kaiser denies unless 3 months of nutrition class plus 2 counseling sessions (BMI > 35). Excellus requires the weight-loss branch only if significant weight loss occurred, so it could approve.
