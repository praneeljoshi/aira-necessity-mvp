# Knee arthroscopy: five payers compared

Documents (all in `sourcing/library/knee-arthroscopy/`): Florida Blue (BCBSFL) MCG 02-20000-65 (rev. 06/15/26); Blue Shield of California BSC7.16 (eff. Dec 1, 2025); Evolent Clinical Guideline 1764 (Nov 2024, impl. July 2025, Louisiana Medicaid copy); Independent Health M20130418034 (NY state products, reviewed 4/1/2026); UnitedHealthcare Community Plan CS068.AD (eff. June 1, 2026). Profiles are in `sourcing/analysis/profiles/`.

## What it is

Knee arthroscopy is a camera-and-instrument procedure inside the knee. The payers treat it not as one service but as a family: diagnostic scope, debridement/chondroplasty, meniscectomy or repair, ligament (ACL/PCL) reconstruction, cartilage restoration, synovectomy, loose body removal, lateral release/patellar realignment and lysis of adhesions. Randomized trials found little benefit of debridement and meniscectomy in degenerative knees, so payers gate mainly on (1) a documented period of failed non-operative care, (2) imaging that confirms a structural target (tear, loose body, cartilage lesion) and (3) the absence of significant arthritis (Kellgren-Lawrence or Outerbridge grade). The five documents differ in how much of this they write down. Florida Blue and Evolent write procedure-by-procedure criteria. Blue Shield CA covers only degenerative tears and chondroplasty in OA. Independent Health only says which OA arthroscopy is not covered. UHC writes no criteria and sends the reader to InterQual.

## Agreement and disagreement

Where they align
- All writing payers refuse arthroscopy for OA with pain alone (Independent Health, BSC I.F, and Florida Blue's debridement requires mechanical symptoms). That echoes Medicare NCD 150.9.
- All require non-operative care first for degenerative disease and all set a ceiling on radiographic arthritis.
- Florida Blue, Evolent and Blue Shield CA all use Kellgren-Lawrence and Outerbridge, with the same grade tables.

Where they differ
- Therapy length: Florida Blue and Evolent agree on 6 weeks for meniscectomy and 3 months / 12 weeks for debridement, chondroplasty, diagnostic arthroscopy and PCL. They split on cartilage microfracture: Florida Blue sets no therapy requirement at all (MRI lesion, pain and/or swelling, mechanical symptoms); Evolent requires 12 weeks and 2 modalities, lesion under 2.0 cm2 and no ligament instability.
- Florida Blue lets injections count as one of the two modalities. Evolent counts 9 modalities and says corticosteroid injection, but also bars cortisone in the last 4 weeks. Florida Blue has no such washout.
- Blue Shield CA's list is "all of the following, unless contraindicated" (six items, some "as appropriate"); Florida Blue and Evolent are 2-of-n. A chart with NSAIDs plus home exercise passes Florida Blue and Evolent but not Blue Shield CA.
- Florida Blue meniscectomy requires "frank (non-degenerative)" tear and K-L 0-2 on X-ray; Blue Shield CA's covered population is degenerative tears with OA and allows K&L up to grade 2 (and Outerbridge grade 3 under 1.5 cm per I.D); Evolent allows K-L 3 only for acute locking with MRI bucket-handle.

Unique to one payer
- Florida Blue only: meniscal allograft transplant criteria (age "e.g., 54 or younger", >50% meniscus loss, <50% joint space narrowing); MISHA experimental; DMARD trial of 6 months for rheumatoid synovectomy (Evolent also says 6 months); plica requires failure of aspiration/steroid injection.
- Evolent only: general requirements for all procedures, cortisone washout, BMI <35, Marx/Tegner activity scoring for ACL, TT-TG >=15 mm, Washington state HTCC limits, cartilage product exclusion list, MUA criteria.
- Blue Shield CA only: relative contraindications (BMI >40, age <50), documentation checklist, acute-event exception.
- Independent Health only: no prior authorization; retrospective review.
- UHC only: WOMAC > 40 definitions; "3 or more moderate to severe radiographic findings" definition of severe OA.

Florida Blue relative to the others
- Stricter: requires MRI-confirmed "frank (non-degenerative)" tear plus K-L 0-2 X-ray for the repairable-tear meniscectomy route (Evolent accepts one of MRI or X-ray, and a surgeon-considered-repairable tear also passes there); requires conservative therapy for bucket-handle tear (6 weeks), where Blue Shield CA and Evolent waive it; diagnostic arthroscopy also requires symptoms for 3 months and inconclusive X-ray and MRI plus 3 months of therapy (Evolent: 12 weeks, same).
- Looser: Florida Blue has no BMI, age, smoking, infection or cortisone conditions in any block; microfracture needs only an MRI lesion, pain and/or swelling, and mechanical symptoms (no therapy, no size cap; Evolent needs lesion <2.0 cm2, 12 weeks therapy, skeletally mature, no ligament instability); pediatric meniscal tears need only MRI and one listed symptom (Evolent: age <21, MRI tear, plus pain, mechanical symptoms or any positive exam finding, so Evolent's cutoff is explicit and Florida Blue's "child or adolescent" is not); loose body removal needs symptoms and imaging only (same as Evolent but Evolent adds cortisone rule).
- Looser than Blue Shield CA on arthritis for debridement: Florida Blue permits arthroscopic debridement in Outerbridge I-II OA with mechanical symptoms and 3 months of therapy; Blue Shield CA requires a meniscal tear on MRI and 6 weeks of therapy but excludes grade 3 and 4.
- Roughly equal to Blue Shield CA on the arthritis ceiling for degenerative disease: Florida Blue's meniscus/ACL/PCL routes need K-L 0-2, Blue Shield CA excludes K&L or Outerbridge grade 3-4.
- Florida Blue is the only payer here that lists a no-longer-covered device (MISHA) by name and HCPCS code.

## Structure

- Layout: Florida Blue and Evolent are bulleted lists per procedure. Blue Shield CA is a short numbered statement with footnoted guidelines. Independent Health is a one-page not-covered list. UHC is a table of codes plus evidence.
- Logic shape: Florida Blue and Evolent are "pathways" (pick the procedure, then all-of). Evolent adds general requirements applied to every pathway, tiered contraindications (absolute versus relative with an unless-clause) and numbered Sections (any-of). Blue Shield CA is one all-of. Independent Health is exclusion-first with an exception list.
- Where criteria live: Florida Blue pp.5-9; grade tables after the criteria; Evolent mixes General Requirements (pp.3-4), per-procedure blocks, Background (pp.20-21) and Washington legislative text; Blue Shield CA splits across Policy Statement and Policy Guidelines footnotes.
- Delegation: UHC fully to InterQual; Florida Blue to Medicare NCDs for MA only and to other MCGs for ACI and arthroplasty; Independent Health to NY State Medicaid; Evolent to Washington HTCC for Washington.
- Codes: all flat lists. None maps codes to criteria blocks. Evolent's CPT lists are grouped by procedure family; Florida Blue's is numerical.
- The Evolent PDF is a stack of three copies of the guideline (84 pages): a redline with struck and inserted words merged, a 27-page copy and a 28-page final. They differ (the redline lysis of adhesions still lists "native knee" and "MUA also performed"; the final does not).

## Problems for an automated checker

1. Pathway selection comes first. Florida Blue and Evolent say nothing global; the checker must map the requested CPT to a block. No code-to-block mapping exists in either document (Florida Blue lists 29880/29881 in a flat table; the same code (meniscectomy) serves "Meniscectomy/Meniscal Repair" and, via chondroplasty included, arthroscopic debridement).
2. The k-of-n therapy menu: "at least 2 of the following: Medications (unless contraindicated); assistive device(s); home exercise; physical therapy, therapeutic injections into the joint; activity modification". Semicolons and commas mix; "physical therapy, therapeutic injections" reads as one item or two. Florida Blue's meniscal block repeats it in three places.
3. "Failure" is undefined everywhere. No visit counts for PT, no dose for medications, and "unless contraindicated" turns a leaf into a conditional.
4. Imaging absence: "No imaging evidence of meniscus tear, loose body, advanced arthritis (Kellgren grade III or IV), or fracture" (Florida Blue, diagnostic). A chart saying nothing about loose bodies is not evidence of absence; the checker must decide INSUFFICIENT versus MET.
5. Grades come from different scales. Blue Shield CA: "Kellgren & Lawrence or Outerbridge Grade 3 greater than 1.5cm or Grade 4"; K-L is on X-ray, Outerbridge is arthroscopic or MRI. A chart may state only one; none says which controls.
6. Internal conflict in Blue Shield CA: I.A requires underlying OA, I.D and II exclude severe OA, and the acute exception in the footnote applies "in the absence of osteoarthritis", so it never reaches the I.A criteria. I.D permits grade 3 under 1.5 cm; II excludes all grade 3.
7. Surgeon-judgement leaves: "MRI evidence of a tear considered repairable by the surgeon" (Florida Blue), "other tear that the requesting physician considers repairable" (Evolent), "inconclusive" X-ray and MRI, "disabling knee pain", "normal knee biomechanics or alignment and stability is present or will be achieved concurrently".
8. Examples vs thresholds: "too young (e.g., age 54 or younger)", "(e.g., ≥15 years)", "(e.g., ≤55 years)". An age 56 chart: fail or judge?
9. Garbled text: Evolent "Two or more or persistent effusion(s)"; Florida Blue "limited ability to straighten the le.g., limping". Extraction quality decides the leaf.
10. Unrecorded in a chart: Marx activity score (>4), Tegner score (>2), WOMAC >40 (UHC definition), TT-TG distance, "no intra-articular cortisone injection within 4 weeks of surgery" (needs dated injection history; absence is silent).
11. Hidden rules: Evolent's Background says only one of MRI or X-ray is required; General Requirements say "Unless otherwise stated in the subsections below"; Blue Shield CA's acute exception is a footnote.
12. UHC cannot be evaluated: "For medical necessity clinical coverage criteria, refer to the InterQual". The definitions (WOMAC > 40, "three or more moderate to severe radiographic findings") are orphans.
13. Independent Health: "Unless there are specific indicators in addition to pain, Independent Health will not cover arthroscopic debridement and lavage of the knee in the context of OA alone", yet the severe-OA rule has no exception; a meniscal disruption in grade III OA is both covered and excluded.
14. Duplicated text: Blue Shield CA Appendix A repeats the policy in a before/after table; Evolent has three copies. A naive extractor produces triplicate criteria.
15. Dates: Blue Shield CA's header says Effective December 1, 2025 while the library JSON records 2024-12-01; Evolent redline shows both old and new dates.

## Test cases

Case A: 58-year-old, medial meniscal tear on MRI described as degenerative with fraying, knee X-ray K-L grade 2, 8 weeks of NSAIDs plus home exercise program, locking episodes. Requests 29881.
- Blue Shield CA: would approve. I.A (OA), I.B (symptoms), I.C (MRI tear), I.D (K-L 2 is not severe), I.E (8 weeks >= 6) satisfied; the Policy Guidelines list "all of" including supervised PT, which a strict reviewer would want, but the statement itself says only "documentation of unsuccessful conservative therapy for at least six weeks".
- Florida Blue: would not approve through the repairable-tear branch because MRI must show "a frank (non-degenerative) meniscus tear" and PT or modalities for >= 6 weeks (2 of 6 is met by NSAIDs plus home exercise, but the tear is degenerative). The "acute injury" branch needs a bucket-handle tear, which this patient lacks. Not approved.
- Evolent: Section Four could pass (>= 2 exam findings or 1 plus mechanical history; MRI frank tear needed in the second alternative; X-ray alternative covers K-L 2): approve if X-ray route is used.

Case B: 40-year-old with a displaced bucket-handle tear on MRI, acute injury 2 weeks ago, locked knee, K-L 0, no therapy yet.
- Evolent: approve (Section Three requires MRI bucket-handle and acute locking; no therapy requirement).
- Blue Shield CA: acute-event footnote waives 6 weeks, but I.A requires underlying OA; reading the exception as available, approve.
- Florida Blue: not approved yet. The acute-injury branch requires "Failure of at least 6 weeks of conservative non-operative management that includes at least 2 of the following".

Case C: 52-year-old with Outerbridge grade II chondral changes, no meniscal tear on MRI, popping and locking, 4 months of PT and NSAIDs, requests arthroscopic debridement (29877).
- Florida Blue: approve ("Osteoarthritis is classified as Outerbridge grade I or II", mechanical symptoms, 3 months with 2 modalities).
- Blue Shield CA: not approved. Policy scope is degenerative tears; I.C needs an MRI meniscal tear.
- Independent Health: arguably not covered unless one of the four indicators exists (loose bodies, unstable flaps, meniscal disruption, impinging osteophytes); locking alone is not on the list.

Case D: 62-year-old with K-L 3 knee, acute locking episode, MRI bucket-handle tear matching tender compartment, 2 weeks of symptoms.
- Evolent: approve; K-L 3 is relatively contraindicated "Unless" acute locking and MRI bucket-handle correlating with compartment.
- Blue Shield CA: not approved (II: grade 3 or 4 NMN by either scale).
- Florida Blue: K-L 3 fails the 0-2 requirement for the repairable branch; the bucket-handle branch has no arthritis ceiling stated but requires 6 weeks of therapy; not approved until 6 weeks pass.
