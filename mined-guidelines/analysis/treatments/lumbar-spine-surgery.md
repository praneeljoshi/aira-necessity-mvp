## What it is

Lumbar spine surgery here means five families of operation: discectomy (remove herniated disc), laminectomy/laminotomy/foraminotomy (open the canal or foramen for stenosis), fusion (join vertebrae, for instability, deformity, prior failed surgery), total disc arthroplasty (artificial disc, in place of fusion), and deformity correction. Payers gate it mainly on (1) a diagnosis with imaging that matches symptoms, (2) a documented failed course of non-operative care (usually PT plus medication, for 6 weeks to 1 year depending on the diagnosis), (3) functional impairment or pain severity, (4) instability or deformity measurements for fusion, and (5) red-flag exceptions (progressive neurologic deficit, cauda equina) that waive the conservative-care step. Only two of the four documents (Florida Blue, Carelon) contain criteria you can put in a tree. The two UHC documents delegate to InterQual, CMS LCDs or another UHC policy.

## Agreement and disagreement

Where they agree. Florida Blue and Carelon both:
- require 6 weeks of conservative care for discectomy and for laminectomy in stenosis, and use VAS 4 as the pain bar for stenosis radicular pain;
- require 3 months for isthmic spondylolisthesis, 6 months for pseudarthrosis timing and 3 months for recurrent herniation timing;
- use age 18-60, 6 months of care and T-score <= -1.0 for disc arthroplasty;
- use Cobb 30 / SVA 5 cm for degenerative scoliosis;
- deny fusion for isolated axial back pain, chronic nonspecific back pain and facet syndrome;
- waive conservative care for progressive neurologic deficit or cauda equina.

Where Florida Blue is stricter than Carelon:
- Conservative-care content. Florida Blue demands prescription-strength analgesics at a therapeutic dose, 6 weeks of PT (or a documented reason PT could not be done), and a behavioral-health evaluation, in every block. Carelon accepts PT plus any one complementary item, including rest or acupuncture, and makes PT optional for disc herniation.
- Fusion for stenosis with instability: 3 months (Florida Blue) vs 6 weeks (Carelon).
- Degenerative scoliosis: 1 year vs 3 months.
- Disc arthroplasty: single level only (Florida Blue) vs two levels with a 2-level FDA implant (Carelon); VAS >= 5 (hard) vs 40/100 "ideally".
- Disc arthroplasty requires an anterior retroperitoneal approach.
- Deformity fusion in adults has a 6-month symptom duration and 12 weeks in the past year of 2 of 4 treatments; Carelon has no equivalent.

Where Florida Blue is looser than Carelon:
- Fusion imaging. Florida Blue accepts "spondylolisthesis demonstrated on plain x-rays" with no millimeter value and sets no recency window; Carelon wants flexion-extension films showing >= 3 mm, the surgeon's own interpretation in the record, and MRI/CT within 6 months for stenosis.
- Idiopathic scoliosis: Florida Blue uses Cobb > 40 for everyone; Carelon requires > 50 for the skeletally mature.
- No annular-closure, staged-fusion, dynamic-stabilization or facet-replacement rules; Carelon and Surest name them. Silence is not coverage, but nothing in the document denies them.
- Adjacent-level disease has its own explicit pathway (3 months of care, imaging patterns, 6 months of relief from the previous fusion); Carelon reaches it only through "adjacent-level stenosis".
- No global severity definition, so "significant functional impairment" is the reviewer's call. Carelon's "3/10 and two ADLs/IADLs" is explicit and arguably easier to meet.

Where Florida Blue is stricter on imaging windows: laminectomy imaging within 6 months, against Carelon's 9 months (herniation) and 12 months (stenosis).

Pathways in one policy but not the other:
- Only Carelon: failed disc arthroplasty, implant failure, synovial cyst, Scheuermann kyphosis, flat back with measured PI-LL mismatch, fusion for same-level herniation without instability after two discectomies, tethered cord, dorsal rhizotomy.
- Only Florida Blue: adjacent-level disease as a standalone fusion diagnosis; an explicit "instability due to fracture, dislocation, infection, abscess, or tumor" fusion pathway is in both, but Florida Blue words it as "when extensive surgery is required that could create an unstable spine".

Unique to one payer:
- Florida Blue: "0-3/5" motor-deficit bands; adult thoracic/lumbar deformity criteria with a 6-month symptom duration; "immediate surgical evaluation" pathway; MA reviewed against NCD 150.10; Florida step-therapy statute note.
- Carelon: global severity definition; independent radiologist rule; supervised home exercise definition; tobacco and shared decision-making recommendations; tethered cord, dorsal rhizotomy, synovial cyst; bone growth stimulator and BMP guidelines in the same file.
- Surest: wholly-defined terms (instability, Meyerding, unstable Grade I) that no visible criterion uses; staged sessions unproven; list of unproven devices.
- UHC MA: nothing clinical; the only lumbar LCD listed is for Palmetto states, and First Coast (Florida) has none in the table.

## Structure

- Layout. Florida Blue: one 16-page policy, evidence summary first (3 pages), then the Position Statement as five procedure blocks. Carelon: eleven stacked guidelines in a 66-page file, each with Description, Clinical Indications, Contraindications, Exclusions, References, Codes; shared definitions repeated at the top of each. Surest: pointer page, definitions, giant CPT table, then 6 pages of evidence. UHC MA: a router with a NCD/LCD table.
- Logic shape. Florida Blue and Carelon are "pathways" (a list of diagnosis blocks) where each block is an ALL list with nested ANY and 1-of-n inside. Florida Blue's fusion list is joined by trailing "OR" lines; Carelon's is a list of headed conditions ("considered medically necessary to treat ANY of the following conditions"). Nesting depth reaches 4 (stenosis fusion: any of three instability bullets AND (a: claudication + imaging + 3 months of 3 therapies, or b: severe motor loss)).
- Delegation. Surest to InterQual subsets (one of them client-defined and hidden); UHC MA to LCDs, to the commercial policy and to InterQual; Florida Blue to NCD 150.10 only for Medicare Advantage; Carelon yields to CMS for MA and to a plan's own policy on request.
- Codes. Lists only, never mapped to criteria. Carelon lists codes per guideline; Florida Blue has one flat list; Surest's list includes facet-replacement and intrafacet codes that match its "unproven" text only by description.
- Cross-references. Carelon: "Also see Lumbar disc herniation in the Lumbar Laminectomy guideline" (two sections hold the same herniation criteria, with different imaging windows: 6 vs 9 months). Florida Blue refers to cervical policy 02-20000-45.

## Problems for an automated checker

- Redline corruption (Carelon). Insertions and deletions both appear as plain text. Examples: "within the past 6 9 months", "within the past 126 months", "and/or or". The History table clarifies (9 months herniation, 12 months stenosis), but a parser reading the body text could read 126 months or 6 months. Mitigation: use the History table or fetch the clean version.
- Open-ended lists. Florida Blue: "no contraindications to lumbar disc arthroplasty, including but not limited to". Carelon: "Indications other than those addressed ... including, but not limited to". A checker cannot enumerate unnamed exclusions.
- Undefined or soft terms. "significant functional impairment", "impairment or loss of function" (no measure in Florida Blue), "a dose sufficient to induce a therapeutic response", "severe or rapidly progressive symptoms", "ideally documented by ... VAS pain score of 40 or higher", "unusual circumstances", "significant relief of symptoms", "All other reasonable sources of pain have been ruled out".
- Vacuous "if any". "Evaluation and appropriate management of associated cognitive, behavioral, or addiction issues, if any" is MET if nothing is charted, and equally if the chart is silent; INSUFFICIENT is the honest answer.
- Scope of AND/OR in Florida Blue stenosis fusion: "Spinal stenosis with one of the following: ... OR ... OR ..., AND Either of the following, a or b". And the doubled "and, AND" in isthmic.
- Waiver inside a criterion. Carelon's "(conservative management requirement waived)" sits inside an EITHER branch, which means a sibling leaf (failure of 6 weeks) must be skipped conditionally.
- "Sole indication" negation (Florida Blue). To judge "experimental if the sole indication is disc herniation" the checker must know whether any other indication exists.
- Per-level evaluation. "criteria must be met for each level intended for fusion" requires splitting one request into level-specific checks, but charts often describe levels loosely.
- Evidence a chart cannot show. Carelon: "surgeon's interpretation of office-based flexion-extension lateral spine x-rays" (must be a specific note); "performed and read by an independent radiologist"; absence of symptomatic DDD at all other levels; "the previous fusion resulted in significant relief for at least 6 months" (patient recall). Florida Blue: failure of "any 2 of the following physician-directed conservative treatments" for 12 consecutive weeks (the chart must show overlapping treatments and continuity); negatives such as "No more than Grade 1 spondylolisthesis demonstrated on x-ray". Surest: instability defined but unused.
- Numbers that look like criteria. Florida Blue evidence section: Cobb 30, 70-75 degrees, 50% facet, 13 years follow-up. Surest evidence: VAS/ODI numbers.
- Duplicated blocks. Florida Blue pastes the same conservative-therapy text five times; Carelon repeats the General Information block eleven times. A naive extractor produces duplicate leaves.
- Different bar for the same word. "Significant pain": Carelon 3/10 + 2 ADLs; Florida Blue VAS 4 daily; TDA VAS 5; Carelon TDA VAS 4/10 "ideally".
- Version drift in Surest. Criteria moved from the standard InterQual lumbar subset to a UHG custom subset on 09/01/2026; the user cannot see it.
- Same threshold, different words: Florida Blue "sagittal vertical axis >5 cm" (degenerative scoliosis) vs "imbalance of at least 5 cm" (deformity, >=). Carelon flat back requires PI-LL > 10 degrees AND SVA > 5 cm, whereas degenerative scoliosis uses SVA > 5 cm OR Cobb >= 30.

## Test cases

Case 1: stenosis with grade 1 spondylolisthesis, 6 weeks of conservative care. A 61-year-old has L4-5 central and lateral recess stenosis, neurogenic claudication limiting walking to one block, MRI 4 months ago, flexion-extension films read by the surgeon showing 4 mm of motion. She has had 6 weeks of PT and NSAIDs and nothing more.
- Carelon: approve. Stenosis fusion needs instability (>= 3 mm flex-ext, surgeon's interpretation documented), neurogenic claudication with functional impairment, 6 weeks of conservative care and MRI within 6 months. All met.
- Florida Blue: do not approve (as written). Stenosis fusion branch a requires "failed at least 3 months of conservative nonsurgical therapy", so 6 weeks fails. Branch b (severe or rapidly progressive motor loss) does not apply.

Case 2: discectomy without formal PT. A 38-year-old has L5-S1 herniation with S1 radiculopathy: positive SLR, dermatomal numbness, daily leg pain at VAS 6, MRI 5 months ago. He had 8 weeks of NSAIDs, a gabapentinoid and one epidural steroid injection, but never attended PT and no reason is documented.
- Carelon: approve. Disc herniation criteria require "Failure of at least 6 weeks of conservative management (Physical therapy optional)" and meet the radicular pain/exam and MRI within 6 months.
- Florida Blue: do not approve. The pain branch requires "Participation in at least 6 weeks of physical therapy (including active exercise) or documentation of why the candidate could not tolerate physical therapy".

Case 3: two-level disc arthroplasty. A 44-year-old has axial discogenic pain, moderate to severe degeneration with Modic changes at L4-5 and L5-S1, VAS 7, 9 months of multifaceted care, normal other levels, and the surgeon plans a 2-level FDA-approved prosthesis.
- Carelon: approve. The guideline allows "single or dual (when using 2-level FDA-approved implant)" levels with age 18-60 and 6 months of care.
- Florida Blue: do not approve as written. The diagnosis bullet requires degeneration "at a single level at L3-4, L4-L5 or L5-S1", which two levels fail. The document is internally inconsistent: a later bullet says arthroplasty "will be performed at one or two consecutive levels from L3-S1", so a reviewer could go either way. This is itself a good test of how the checker handles contradictory leaves.

Case 4 (optional): degenerative scoliosis, Cobb 32 degrees, persistent axial pain with progression, 6 months of PT and injections. Carelon: approve (3 months). Florida Blue: do not approve (needs 1 year).
