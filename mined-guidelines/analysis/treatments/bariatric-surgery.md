# Bariatric surgery: four payers compared

Documents: Florida Blue 02-40000-10 (rev 02/15/26, 22 pp); Cigna 0051 (eff 7/15/2026, 111 pp); Humana Healthy Horizons Louisiana Medicaid LA.CLI.038.001 (eff 05/18/2026, 7 pp); UnitedHealthcare Community Plan Ohio CS007OH (43 pp; the PDF text says version E, eff 07/01/2026, while the library metadata says CS007OH.D / 05/01/2025).

## What it is

Bariatric (weight-loss) surgery changes the stomach and sometimes the small intestine to restrict intake or absorption: sleeve gastrectomy, Roux-en-Y gastric bypass, adjustable gastric band, biliopancreatic diversion with duodenal switch, and newer variants. Payers gate it on a BMI threshold (usually 40, or 35 with a comorbidity), a documented failure of non-surgical weight loss, a psychological/nutrition work-up, and age band (adult vs adolescent). They also keep a list of "experimental" operations and endoscopic devices, and have separate rules for revision/conversion. Plan contracts may exclude the benefit entirely, and every policy says so.

## Agreement and disagreement

Agreement: all payers allow adults at BMI >= 40 and, with a comorbidity, at lower BMIs; all require some documented failed weight-loss effort and a mental-health input; all allow revision for major complications; all treat balloons, TORe, mini/OAGB and VBLOC as experimental or unproven (Cigna and Humana differ on balloons only by silence in Humana).

Where Florida Blue is stricter:
- Adult no-comorbidity entry is BMI 40; Cigna allows 35 with no comorbidity, and 27.5 for Asian patients with attestation.
- Class II (35-39.9) requires a comorbidity "refractory to medical management"; Cigna class 2 needs none, and Cigna admits BMI 30-34.9 with a comorbidity. Humana admits 35 with a comorbidity and 30-34.9 with uncontrolled T2DM. Florida Blue calls surgery for T2DM at BMI < 35 investigational.
- Procedure scope is narrower: SADI-S, SIPS, ESG, OAGB, long-limb bypass and two-stage staging are investigational; Cigna covers SADI-S, stand-alone or staged sleeve, and endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty (43889).
- Florida Blue requires the patient to have no medically treatable cause for obesity; no other payer here states it.
- Adolescent requires Tanner 4-5 and near-final height plus family-unit psychological stability; Cigna and Humana state none of these.
- Revision for stretching from overeating is explicitly excluded, but without a two-year failure rule (Cigna's route).

Where Florida Blue is looser:
- No multidisciplinary evaluation, no nutrition evaluation, no "within 12 months" window, and psychological evaluation is "with counseling as needed", not "unequivocal clearance" (Cigna).
- No duration for non-surgical attempts (as for Cigna and Humana) and no pregnancy counseling or adherence determination (Humana).
- Revision for complications has no time gate; Cigna's non-complication route needs two years.
- Adolescent age floor not stated (Cigna 11, Humana 13).

Unique to one payer: Humana's pregnancy-avoidance counseling, its "more than 100 lbs overweight" route, and a panniculectomy-after-bariatric section inside the same policy (18 months, stable weight 6 months, pannus at/below the pubic symphysis, plus skin problem or functional limit); Cigna's Asian BMI cutoffs, liver biopsy/EGD rules, and open vs laparoscopic code table; Florida Blue's Tanner staging; UHC's complete delegation to InterQual.

## Structure

Florida Blue: classic MCG layout (Description, Position Statement, Billing/Coding with LOINC table, Program Exceptions); flat bullets using OR/AND at line ends; experimental list; revision in the same statement. Cigna: criteria at the front, long evidence review behind (pp.13-78 of 111); procedure tables carry the code mapping; separate adult and adolescent branches, each with initial and revision subsections. Humana: a seven-page Medicaid template; a shared gate (evaluation) applies to all, then age-banded pathways; it embeds a panniculectomy policy. UHC: a "navigation" document: Coverage Rationale is a one-line pointer to InterQual and a NOT list; everything else is background (the Clinical Evidence section is ~25 pages).

Logic shapes: Florida Blue and Cigna are AND of [OR of two BMI paths, plus evaluation items]; Cigna nests a second OR (comorbidity list) and an ALL-of-4 evaluation; Humana is ALL of (evaluation) AND (age pathway), with ORs and open catch-all comorbidity; revisions are OR of "initial criteria again" or "major complication".

## Problems for an automated checker

- UHC has no criteria text. A checker could only apply the unproven list; InterQual would have to be licensed or transcribed. Library metadata labels the file D/2025 while the PDF is E/2026: check document identity before trusting a profile.
- "Has made multiple attempts at non-surgical weight loss" (Florida Blue): no count or length; a checker can only decide whether the chart shows any attempts.
- "refractory to medical management" (Florida Blue comorbidity) versus Cigna "poorly controlled hypertension (systolic ... at least 140 ... despite optimal medical management)": the first has no threshold, the second does.
- "unequivocal clearance for bariatric surgery by a mental health provider" (Cigna): charts usually say "cleared"; "unequivocal" is a judgement.
- Humana's evaluation items are "A determination that..." made by the team; a chart may hold the fact but not the determination.
- "BMI ... more than 100 lbs overweight" (Humana): reference weight undefined.
- Humana's unbalanced parenthesis: "(eg, stroke, myocardial infarction, poorly controlled hypertension (systemic blood pressure greater than 140 mm Hg or diastolic blood pressure 90 mm Hg or greater" and the flat line "Cardiovascular disease ... OR History of cardiomyopathy" may parse as nested.
- Florida Blue OR/AND at line ends (BMI OR ..., AND does not have ..., AND multiple attempts); a parser must attach the ANDs to both BMI branches.
- Open comorbidity lists ("including but not limited to"; "any other comorbidity ... improved by weight loss").
- Asian BMI cutoffs need a documented attestation, which is not a chart fact.
- Unknown criteria in revisions: Florida Blue "e.g." list; Cigna "ANY other indication" swallows the list.
- Evidence sections (Cigna 60+ pages, UHC 25+) contain BMI/AHI numbers that are not criteria and will pollute regex extraction.
- Weight at the time of the evaluation vs surgery date: Cigna says "within the previous 12 months", with no statement whether BMI must be measured then.

## Test cases

1. 42-year-old, BMI 37.5, no comorbidity, 12-month physician-supervised diet, cleared by psychologist and dietitian within 6 months, requests sleeve gastrectomy. Cigna approves (BMI >= 35 branch, 4-part evaluation done). Florida Blue denies (class II requires at least one refractory comorbidity). Humana and UHC cannot be judged from the PDF (Humana needs BMI >= 35 plus a comorbidity: also not met).
2. 35-year-old, BMI 32.8, T2DM with HbA1c 8.9 on metformin and insulin, requests Roux-en-Y. Humana approves (BMI 30-34.9 with T2DM and hyperglycemia inadequately controlled despite medication). Cigna approves (30-34.9 with diabetes). Florida Blue denies (BMI < 35 is not eligible; surgery for T2DM at BMI < 35 is investigational).
3. 50-year-old, BMI 44, sleeve gastrectomy 3 years ago, 8% regain, no complication, requests conversion to bypass. Florida Blue denies (revision covered only for listed complications or documented dilation with compliance). Cigna approves (weight-loss failure >= 2 years, initial criteria met, RYGB on the list, no noncompliance). Humana approves via "Initial bariatric surgery requirements are met" if the evaluation is current.
4. 16-year-old, BMI 41, Tanner 3, requests sleeve. Cigna and Humana (age 13-17) could approve if evaluation done; Florida Blue denies without Tanner 4 or 5.
