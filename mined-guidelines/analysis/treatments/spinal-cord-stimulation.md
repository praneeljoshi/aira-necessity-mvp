# Spinal cord stimulation: cross-payer analysis

Documents profiled (in `sourcing/analysis/profiles/`): Florida Blue 02-61000-05 (SCS and DRG), Excellus 7.01.51 (14 pp), Premera 7.01.546 (33 pp), UnitedHealthcare Commercial 2026T0567DD (13 pp), UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage MMP368 (13 pp).

## What it is

Spinal cord stimulation (SCS) places epidural leads connected to a pulse generator to interrupt chronic pain signaling. It is done in two stages: a temporary trial (days to a couple of weeks) and, if the trial gives enough relief, permanent implantation. Dorsal root ganglion (DRG) stimulation is a variant aimed at the DRG. Payers gate it on (1) a pain diagnosis (neuropathic: failed back surgery syndrome, CRPS, painful diabetic neuropathy; not nociceptive or central pain), (2) failure of conservative care, (3) a psychological or behavioral health clearance, and (4) at least 50 percent relief in the trial before the permanent implant. Replacement/revision rules and the investigational list (angina, critical limb ischemia, cancer pain, heart failure) differ widely.

## Agreement and disagreement

**Where payers align**
- Two stages: trial then permanent, with at least 50 percent relief as the gate (all five; UHC MA adds an analgesic-reduction alternative and functional improvement; UHC Commercial defers to InterQual).
- Conservative therapy failure or unsuitability, last-resort language, and a psychological gate appear in four of five with similar wording.
- Refractory angina is investigational or unproven everywhere except Excellus (CCS III/IV pathway). Cancer pain and heart failure are excluded where mentioned.
- Replacing a functioning standard SCS with high-frequency is not necessary (Excellus, Premera).

**Where they differ**
- Diagnosis scope: Excellus is a closed list of four indications (and excludes diabetic neuropathy, phantom limb pain); Florida Blue and Premera use an open "neuropathic, damaged nerves" definition with examples including diabetic neuropathy and phantom limb pain; UHC Commercial limits to CRPS, painful diabetic neuropathy and FBSS; UHC MA is a general chronic-pain NCD and allows some nociceptive pain.
- Conservative therapy duration: Excellus is the only one that states a number (six consecutive months). Others have none.
- Psych gate: Florida Blue requires documentation that "the pain is not psychological in origin"; Excellus requires an attestation by a behavioral health provider; Premera requires clearance plus no untreated drug habituation; UHC MA requires screening by a multidisciplinary team.
- Trial duration: Excellus ">48 hours", Premera "at least a 3-day trial", Florida Blue none.
- Excellus alone gives full objective criteria for CRPS (Budapest-like k-of-n), critical limb ischemia (Rutherford, mmHg) and angina (CCS, medical therapy goals).
- Premera alone has site-of-service rules.
- DRG: investigational at Excellus; CRPS-only at UHC Commercial; covered like SCS at Florida Blue, Premera, UHC MA.

**Florida Blue vs the others**
- Looser: no conservative-therapy duration (Excellus six months); no trial length; no condition-specific objective criteria (Excellus's CRPS, CLI and angina requirements); DRG covered for the same broad population (UHC limits to CRPS; Excellus investigational); open-ended diagnosis list including diabetic neuropathy and phantom limb pain (Excellus investigational for both); ICD-10 list includes broad pain codes.
- Stricter: the psychological evaluation must state the pain is not psychological in origin ("and/or" with no uncontrolled mental health problem), a documentation burden Premera and UHC MA lack in this form; requires FDA approval of device; revision/removal reimbursed only if the original implant was allowed and device malfunctions (Premera also covers replacement when the condition changed). Florida Blue explicitly calls nociceptive and central pain "generally not effective" (Premera lists them investigational; UHC MA is more permissive about nociceptive pain). UHC MA accepts 50% analgesic reduction as an alternative to 50% pain relief, Florida Blue requires pain relief only. Florida Blue lacks the cardiology/vascular pathways Excellus offers: angina and CLI are investigational at Florida Blue but covered with criteria at Excellus.
- Similar: Florida Blue and Premera have nearly the same last-resort/neuropathic/psych structure (both reflect the BCBSA 7.01.25 reference).

## Structure

- **Layout**: Florida Blue is a one-page position statement with a trial block and a permanent block; documentation in Reimbursement Information; huge CPT/HCPCS/ICD list. Excellus has 12 Roman-numbered statements with sub-levels to four deep (I.A.1.b.i) and definition tables (Rutherford, CCS). Premera is a two-column table (site of service and procedure rows) in a web-style layout. UHC Commercial is a whitelist plus a link to InterQual; the rest is evidence. UHC MA is prose, derived from NCD 160.7, with a state-by-MAC LCD table.
- **Logic**: Florida Blue and Premera: ALL-of with one OR in the failed-modalities bullet; permanent = trial criteria AND 50 percent relief. Excellus: pathways by indication with k-of-n inside CRPS (3 of 4 symptom categories, 2 of 4 sign categories). UHC Commercial: nothing to model. UHC MA: staged prose, with "or" inside the 50% bullet.
- **Criteria location**: Excellus splits (six-month duration and objective 50% documentation only in Policy Guidelines; Rutherford/CCS definitions in Description). Premera splits site of service and clinical criteria into stacked tables; definitions at the end. UHC Commercial: elsewhere (InterQual, proprietary).
- **Delegation**: UHC Commercial to InterQual; UHC MA to NCD 160.7 and state LCDs; Florida Blue MA to NCD 160.7; Premera to a site-of-service policy. 
- **Codes**: 63650, 63655, 63685, 63688 etc. appear in all; HCPCS L8679-L8695; Excellus tags C1826 (E/I); ICD-10 lists exist only at Florida Blue (broad pain list) and none for others.
- **Versions**: The UHC MA PDF prints MMP368.14 (effective 9/1/2026) but the library file/metadata say MMP368.11 (06/01/2024); trust the printed text.

## Problems for an automated checker

- **Vague core leaves**: Florida Blue "used as a last resort", "tried and failed", "severe and chronic, intractable" have no thresholds. "other treatment modalities ... tried and failed OR ... judged to be unsuitable" lets a clinician statement satisfy it, and a chart cannot prove "last resort".
- **and/or**: Florida Blue "documentation clearly states the pain is not psychological in origin and/or no evidence of an uncontrolled mental health problem". Excellus conservative therapy list "pharmacotherapy, physical therapy, cognitive therapy, and activity lifestyle modification" (FBSS) versus "cognitive behavioral therapy, or activity lifestyle modification" (CRPS): same concept, opposite connective. Excellus "Surgical intervention is not indicated, or the patient does not wish to proceed" makes patient preference a bypass.
- **Open lists**: "Common indications include, but are not limited to" (Florida Blue, Premera) vs closed lists (Excellus). A checker cannot decide membership for a diagnosis not named.
- **Hedged exclusions**: "Spinal cord stimulation is generally not effective in treating nociceptive pain" (Florida Blue) is a rationale, not a clear NOT. Premera's lists nociceptive pain as investigational. Chart diagnosis codes (e.g., M54.5x low back pain, listed as supporting at Florida Blue) conflict with the nociceptive exclusion.
- **Hidden criteria**: UHC Commercial's criteria sit in InterQual; nothing to extract. UHC MA's binding criteria may sit in the LCD for the member's MAC (First Coast for Florida).
- **Attestations a chart rarely shows**: behavioral-health attestation, vascular-surgeon "not a suitable candidate", cardiologist attestation, "multidisciplinary team" evaluation, clearance.
- **Cross-reference to definitions**: Excellus Rutherford grade II category 4 and CCS class III/IV require tables in the Description section; Premera "Poorly controlled, resistant hypertension*" needs a footnote ("3 or more drugs").
- **Extraction**: Premera's two-column table with repeated headers and mid-bullet page breaks; Excellus four-level nesting and mid-list page breaks; UHC MA 'Applicable Codes' diagnosis list is a link; Excellus ICD10 section says only "Multiple diagnosis codes".
- **Staging**: "permanent" criteria say "all criteria for temporarily implanted stimulator are met" (Florida Blue) — the checker must evaluate the trial tree first and then the response.

## Test cases

1. **52-year-old with painful diabetic neuropathy in both feet, 8 months of gabapentin and duloxetine, PT, psych clearance, requests SCS trial.** Florida Blue and Premera approve (peripheral/painful diabetic neuropathy is a named neuropathic indication; modalities tried; psych cleared). UHC Commercial approves the diagnosis (painful diabetic neuropathy) subject to InterQual. Excellus denies: peripheral neuropathy (e.g., diabetic) is investigational (VII.C).
2. **61-year-old with CCS class III angina, not a revascularization candidate, on antiplatelet, statin, beta blocker, BP controlled, behavioral health attestation of no issues.** Excellus approves the trial under IV.A (cardiologist attestation, optimal medical therapy). Florida Blue, Premera and UHC Commercial deny: refractory angina is investigational or unproven.
3. **45-year-old with FBSS, trial gave 40% pain relief but 60% reduction in opioid use and improved walking, requests permanent implant.** UHC Medicare Advantage approves: "50% reduction of analgesic medications" plus functional improvement is enough. Florida Blue (at least 50% pain relief), Excellus (at least 50% pain reduction) and Premera (50% reduction in pain) deny the permanent implant because the trial pain reduction is below 50%.
4. **(Optional) 38-year-old with FBSS, 3 months of NSAIDs/PT, wants a trial.** Excellus denies (six consecutive months of supervised conservative care required); Florida Blue and Premera could approve if the physician documents modalities as "tried and failed" or unsuitable, since neither states a duration.
